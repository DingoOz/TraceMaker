Detailed Description

Gridless legalisation of routed copper (doc 05 §41.3, D104).

The lattice router places every track on a lattice point and cannot move one it has committed, so a solution found under looser rules (smaller clearances) usually has room to spare that the lattice cannot use. This module takes such routes and moves the vertices of their tracks and vias (the topology, layers and order along every channel stay as they are) until every clearance of the board's real rules holds, or as many as it can: a vertex is pushed along the normal of each pair of copper items that are too close, by Gauss-Seidel sweeps over the pairs (position-based dynamics, Müller, Heidelberger, Hennix and Ratcliff, J. Vis. Commun. Image Represent. 18(2), 2007). A prototype that solved the same problem by sequential linear programming (HiGHS, minimum total movement) did about as well (6 stuck srj20 boards, 0.06 mm solutions to 0.10 mm: LP 3 legal and 3-11 pairs left on the others, projection 2 legal and 2-9 left). What remains violated is dropped and routed again by the caller, and every route that is kept passes the router's exact check, so the heuristic can lose work but never legality.

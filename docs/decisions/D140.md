# D140 — Unfilled zones are filled for routing; the router keeps custom hole clearance

## Context

A 1,194-connection, four-layer WholeBoard board (PoE switch, every part on F.Cu, GND and +54V zones on In1/In2 written
without fills, JLCPCB rules) routed 1,184 connections in two hours. The user saw it use too few vias and layers. The
routed board showed why: the zones had no fill, so the router routed GND and +54V as tracks (1,773 mm, part of it on the
other net's plane layer) instead of dropping vias into the planes, and those wide tracks crowded the fine-pitch escapes
where the open connections were. KiCad's DRC of the same board also found 14 `hole_clearance` errors: the router ignored
the custom "Hole to copper" rule (0.254 mm). Doc 05 §54.

## Decision

- Zones without a fill are filled in memory before routing and the run uses soft zones (plane targets, refill after
  routing). On by default; `route --no-fill-planes` turns it off. The written zones stay as they were.
- `RuleEngine::router_hole_clearance()`: the board minimum raised to every applied custom `hole_clearance` rule, used by
  every router check. Rules with a condition or layer are applied everywhere, with a warning.

## Alternatives

- **Ask for `--soft-zones`.** It does not help alone: a zone with no fill has no area, so it is no plane target.
- **Write the fills into the output.** KiCad refills on its own; leaving the zones as written keeps rule 8.
- **Per-pair hole clearance in the router.** Exact for conditional rules, but every cache and margin in
  `route::Obstacles` assumes one value; the floor is exact for unconditional rules, which is the common case (JLCPCB).

## Consequences

- Boards without unfilled zones or custom hole-clearance rules route as before (quick tier byte-identical, 30/30).
- Soft zones bring their costs: the refill and refill repair after routing take time on large boards.
- The full-board result on the switch board (one hour, same options) was not measured before this was shipped.
- Open: conditions using `A.Net` are not evaluated (JLCPCB's "Hole to hole").

# D171 — Decoupling caps without a real input position are spread over their IC's supply pins

## Context

Issue #55. On WholeBoard's 10-port PoE switch, `tracemaker-place --scratch` put 22-25 of the 29 decoupling caps of
each KSZ9897 (TQFP-128, 27 supply pins on four sides) on one side of the chip. That is the side where port 5's MDI
pairs and the RGMII bus leave. D25 ties each cap to the IC supply pad nearest the cap *in the input*. From scratch
the input is a pile (WholeBoard drops the parts to the right of the board), so every cap is tied to the pins that
face the pile. Doc 04 §2 already planned an assignment of caps to pins, not nearest-pin ties. Records:
`bench/records/issue55/`.

## Decision

- `crules::spread_unplaced_decaps` re-ties the caps whose input position carries no intent. A cap counts as
  unplaced when it is movable and either `--scratch` is given or its body lies wholly outside the board outline's box
  (as KiCad drops new parts). Per supply net, in ascending net order, each unplaced cap (in footprint order) goes to
  the IC (U*, IC*) pad on that net with the fewest caps tied to it so far. Equal loads are taken in bit-reversed
  order of the pad list (van der Corput), so any number of caps lands evenly round the package and across the ICs on
  the net. The designer's caps keep their nearest pad and count towards that pad's load. Integer and deterministic.
- When the IC has 48 or more pads (`kDecapStandoffPins`), the tie's IC end is not the pad. It is the point
  `--decap-standoff-mm` (default 2 mm) outside the IC's body, straight out from the pad across the nearer body side,
  so the band next to the pin rows stays free for escapes and fan-out vias. 2 mm keeps the cap within DEC-01's 5 mm
  (PoE: max 4.9 mm).
- **Off by default** (`--decap-spread` turns it on; `ExtractOptions::decap_spread`). Off is byte-identical to before.

## Alternatives

- Spreading onto the pins themselves (standoff 0): the caps are closest (PoE median 1.7 mm), but every pin row gets a
  cap against it. It routed 91 fewer connections on the PoE board and 41 / 60 fewer on the public piles.
- A no-place band along the escape edges of fine-pitch chips: the band would have to move with the IC in full mode,
  which needs a new legality term. The standoff gets the same effect from the objective alone.
- Caps on the bottom side under their pins: the issue's own trial routed 1145 of 1189, worse than the one-side cluster,
  because the caps take the fan-out vias' place (and it makes assembly double-sided).
- A Hungarian (min-cost) assignment on input distances: from a pile, the distances only say which pins face the pile.
  This is the same bias, so nothing is left to optimise once the input carries no intent.
- On by default for `--scratch`: not a clear win (below).

## Consequences

- PoE switch (`--mode full --scratch`, routed at `--work 10000000`): the caps go to all four sides in proportion to
  the supply pins (U10: 9/9/4/11 against pins 9/7/3/8; before 1/25/1/2). Routed 993 of 1,189, against 960 before and
  869 when spread onto the pins.
- Eight public PCBench boards with a 64-154-pin IC, piled (`--work 1000000`, two seeds): the busiest side holds
  about half of a chip's caps instead of 0.6-0.7. Routed 1549 vs 1581 (seed 1) and 1582 vs 1578 (seed 2) of 2,146,
  with single boards swinging by up to ±48. No board is clean at that budget in any variant.
- Because the routing change is mixed, it stays an option. WholeBoard can pass `--decap-spread` with `--scratch`.
  Still open: a cap goes to a pad on its net on any IC, not the IC whose schematic sheet it is on (there are no sheets
  in the board file); the standoff side is the nearer body side, which suits QFP/QFN rows more than BGA inner balls;
  the crules DEC-01 pseudo-nets and report still use nearest-pin (they are skipped for caps D25 tied).
- Doc 04 §2 updated; `bench/run.py` honours `TM_PLACE_BINARY`, as `bench/place_m8.py` does.

# D172 — Repair windows lift the unlocked copper near open connections

## Context

Issue #56: `tracemaker route` without `--reroute` keeps every track and via of the input as fixed copper. On a WholeBoard PoE switch
routed 1186/1189 the last pins stayed open ("fixed copper encloses the pin"): the neighbours' fan-outs wall them in and may not be
ripped. The only alternative was `--reroute`, a fresh two-hour run. A contributor hit the same failure in a staged flow (differential
pairs first with `--skip-net`, the rest second) and asked for `--only-net` / `--skip-net` to be documented and to take patterns.
Doc 05 §73; records in `bench/records/issue56/`.

## Decision

- **Repair windows** (`passes/repair.{hpp,cpp}`, run by `app/route_job.cpp` after the rescue and the advisor). For each connection still
  open, the *branches* of other nets with copper within r of either end pad (from the pad's edge) are lifted. A branch is the chain of
  tracks and vias of one net between two stops (a pad, a zone fill, an arc or copper graphic, or a junction of three or more tracks and
  vias); copper the lift leaves dead goes too. The board is routed again by one router, the rest fixed, the open connection first, on
  `--repair-work` (3 M) work.
- The attempt is a transaction (rule 4): kept only if no two pads joined before come apart and fewer connections are open. Otherwise
  the window grows (`--repair-window-mm`, default 1, 2, 4). `--repair-branches` (64) ends the growth, `--repair-conns` (64) bounds the
  connections tried, `--time` is a safety limit only.
- Never lifted: locked items, tuning patterns, free vias, branches touching arcs or copper graphics, the connection's own net, nets the
  run does not route, coupled pairs and bus members.
- **Default `--repair auto`**: only on a board that came with unlocked tracks or vias, and only when connections are left open. `on` also
  repairs boards routed from nothing here; `off` never.
- The lifted copper leaves the written board and is listed under `removed` in `--emit-items`; the KiCad plugin removes it in the same
  commit, and changes nothing if an item is missing from the open board.
- `--only-net` / `--skip-net` are listed options and take patterns (KiCad wildcards or regular expressions; repeatable or comma separated).
  The implementation is outside `route/router.cpp` (issue #67 changes rip-up there at the same time).

## Alternatives

- Cut the tracks within a radius (the user's manual attempt): it left 11 open instead of 3, since the cut ends stay as fixed stubs.
- Lift whole copper trees per net (the first version): on the PoE switch one 1 mm window lifted 270 tracks, since power trees span the
  board.
- Hand the input's copper to the router as its own routes, so negotiation may rip it anywhere: a designer's copper does not split into
  the router's planned connections, the rip-up would not be limited to a window, and it would change `router.cpp`.
- On by default for every board: on a fresh board the router can already rip its own copper, and D155's pin rescue (the same idea on
  routed copper) lost against the same work spent on routing. `auto` keeps fresh boards byte-identical (the quick tier's inputs have no
  tracks).

## Consequences

- Staged flow on nine PCBench quick-tier boards (stage 2 with the stage-1 copper fixed, 5 M work, one variant): 446 of 487 routed plain,
  484 with the repair (never worse), 476 when the plain run gets the repair's work instead; KiCad's DRC adds no error.
- With the default flow (relax rescue on): 467 with `--repair off`, 486 with `auto`.
- The issue's own board is not finished: at the KSZ9897 QFNs every window holds 9-40 branches of fan-out copper, and no attempt routed
  them all again (doc 05 §73).
- A partly routed board left open pays up to `--repair-conns` x radii router runs; complete boards and fresh boards pay nothing.

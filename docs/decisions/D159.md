# D159 — Thin tracks, real vias: the cut test of a routed topology

## Context

The plan for a router that always completes a routable board asked whether a board could be routed with impossibly thin tracks and the
tracks thickened afterwards. Relax, route, legalise (D104, doc 05 §41.3) does this at 60 % of the clearance. It completed srj20 boards that
hours of search had not, but it lost 509 connections on the 33 failing PCBench tier B-D boards (doc 10 §11.3). Nothing could tell whether
a relaxed solution's topology fits under the real rules (so the legaliser failed) or cannot fit (so it had to fail).

## Decision

- **The cut test** (`route/cut_test.{hpp,cpp}`, `tracemaker cuts`). It checks the cut condition of Cole and Siegel and of Leiserson and
  Maley (STOC 1985) on every copper layer of a routed board: for every two features in sight of each other, up to 2 mm apart, the wires
  that cross the segment between them must fit with their widths and the rule clearances. It also reports the clearance scale s\* that
  the topology allows, and the vias that have no room anywhere in their cell (a proof that the topology cannot be legalised).
- **The legaliser models pads as they are.** A pad at an angle that is not a multiple of 90° is a rotated rectangle, not its bounding box.
  An oval and a rounded rectangle are a rectangle grown by their corner radius. An offset pad's copper is centred where its copper is.
  This is the default with `--relax` and `--legalize`; nothing else calls the legaliser. `TM_LEGALIZE_BBOX_PADS=1` restores the bounding
  boxes, for experiments.
- **Two options, off by default:**
  - `--ramp-vias-real`: relaxed stages lower the clearance of tracks only (`DesignRules::via_clearance_extra`);
  - `--ramp-cut-prune`: before legalising, drop the routes the cut test proves cannot be legalised. These are the routes whose vias have no
    room, and enough of the routes through each over-full cut between two pads (a greedy cover).

## Alternatives

- **An exact realizer** (shortest homotopic paths with spacing, the "rubber band" sketch) in place of the projection legaliser. Not built in
  this change. Once pads were modelled as they are, the projection keeps every relaxed route at the first hand-over on d20 and poncho. On
  srj20 its leftovers sit at vias the cut test proves have no room, which no realizer could place. It remains the next step for the final
  hand-over: on poncho the cuts say there is room (s\* 1.167) and the projection still leaves 71 pairs.
- **A larger move box or more sweeps** for the projection legaliser (`TM_LEGALIZE_BOX=0.5`, `TM_LEGALIZE_SWEEPS=2000`). These changed
  little on d20 and poncho, and the larger box did worse on poncho.
- **`--legalize-subdivide 0.1` on by default under `--relax`.** +30 connections on the 33 PCBench boards (12 better, 1 worse), but
  neutral on srj20's six stuck boards (404 and 404 of 453) for about 10 % more CPU. Left as an option.
- **Vias at real clearance by default.** Stage 1 then routes fewer connections, and no srj20 board completed.

## Consequences

- `tracemaker cuts` gives a cheap (about 0.3 s per board) necessary check of a routed topology, with no false alarm on 405 clean boards.
- `--relax` on boards with rotated, oval or rounded pads keeps its relaxed routes through the hand-overs instead of losing a third of them at
  each one. d20_tri's first hand-over kept 274 of 427 routes before and 427 of 427 after.
- On the 33 failing PCBench tier B-D boards, at equal work against the current base, `--relax` loses 241 connections instead of 483. It
  completes five boards against the base's two. The better of the two per board gives +42 and five clean boards. Relax is still a rescue,
  not a default.
- On srj20 neither option changed the result (048: 72/74 in all three arms; 072: 75, 74, 75 of 81). The thin sketch's limit there is via
  room in ball-grid cells.
- No DRC error added in any arm. The quick tier is byte-identical to main, and srj20 relax output is unchanged (axis-aligned rectangular
  pads).
- Open: s\* and the cuts are a measure of room that the router could use while it routes (thin geometry, thick accounting), not only after.
- Docs: doc 05 §67, records in `bench/records/d159-thin-cut`.

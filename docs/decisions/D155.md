# D155 — Completing hard boards: proof-triggered pin rescue first

## Context

The user asked how to get hard boards to 100 % routed. By eye they saw vias placed too close to a footprint's pins, connections that
would route with two vias and a run on another layer, and (on a 5-port PoE switch) paths that must first go the other way through several
vias. They proposed that the last few connections get much wider, nearly randomised search settings. A research round read the
literature (escape routing, pin access, multi-layer search, negotiation), the source of Freerouting, tscircuit's capacity autorouter and
KiCad's PNS router, and measured 349 open connections on seven failing boards of the 0.12.1 release run, each routed alone on a copy of its
final board under controlled changes. Report and records: `bench/records/m22-research/` (doc 18 §18).

The measurements:
- The A* already searches every layer, with a via possible at every lattice point and a via-aware cost-to-go field. "Boxed in" is an exact
  proof over all layers.
- 139 of the 349 route alone on the final board, most with 0-2 vias. They were never attempted (152 in the run), ripped and never re-routed,
  or proved boxed in during a strict pass that never negotiated. Four of the seven boards spent no work in negotiation.
- Other nets' copper within 2 mm of the pin rescues 155 of 288 hard cases. Most of that copper is tracks: the 43 cases rescued by removing
  only nearby vias also route with only the tracks removed.
- A whole-board window rescues 78, mostly long detours (Aleste `/RAM/MA1`: 25.4 mm straight, 217.5 mm with 4 vias).
- About 15 % are enclosed even with the whole board and every option.

## Decision

Milestone M22, "Finish the last connections", in the report's order, each change behind an option that is off until it is measured:

1. Proof-triggered pin rescue. When a strict search proves an end boxed in by this run's routed copper, rip the routed connections with
   copper within about 2 mm of that pin, route the boxed connection first, then the victims strictly, as a transaction kept only when the
   routed count rises. On failure, escalate with a seeded victim order and a growing radius, with a nogood per (pin, radius).
2. Leave nothing unattempted. When the main loop ends, retry every ripped-not-re-routed and never-attempted connection on the current
   copper, with a per-connection cap.
3. One whole-board attempt for searches that end at the window edge, never for proved enclosures.
4. Informed pad-to-plane searches: an admissible bound (distance to the net's fill plus a via when off the plane layer), the late
   reachability proof, and planning to nearby same-net copper when it is closer. `tracemaker causes` fills and softens zones as `route` does.
5. Gate the D153 via retry on budget misses, against a control with unchanged prices and the same extra budget.
6. The escape plan in more variants, then a via-only cost in dense pins' outward cones.
7. A tight via state and off-lattice via sites.
8. Research, only if dense-package fan-out is still the largest residual class: a via-site and layer planner (pin access points, via
   exclusion, crossing and layer-change planning).

The user's "widen and randomise" becomes item 1's escalation: the radius and the victim order widen around one proved-stuck pin, seeded.
The search costs themselves are not randomised.

Gate: at least 15 % fewer open connections on `pcbench-bd-fail` at 10 M work, judged by KiCad; no clean board lost on the clean tier B-D
boards; `quick` and `mid` byte-identical with the options off; `quick` once at the end with them on.

## Alternatives

- **A new layer-aware heuristic.** The field already prices vias on every layer; nothing in the literature read is stronger.
- **Cheaper vias everywhere.** They rescued 40 connections, a subset of the 78 that extra work alone rescues; srj20 at via cost 1.0 lost.
- **Moving vias after routing, on its own.** The via's tracks block as much as the via. It remains possible inside item 1, where a via
  moves with its stub.
- **Randomised search costs and weighted A*.** Measured before and lost (doc 16 §11; weighted A* 1.5: 4 % clean on srj20).
- **A fixed budget share for repair** (`--polish`, stopping the strict pass at 70 %). It lost every time it was tried: the share is taken from
  connections that could use it. Repair is triggered by a proof instead.
- **Learned or LLM routers.** PCBWorld's results and the vendors' self-reported numbers give no completion engine to adopt.

## Consequences

The plan is in doc 18 §18 and the roadmap (doc 11, M22). The study harness (`viastudy.cpp` and scripts) and its per-connection results
are kept in `bench/records/m22-research/viastudy/`, so each change can be measured connection by connection. The PoE switch's two
connections enclosed by WholeBoard's locked copper cannot be routed by any change here; the run report should say "enclosed by locked
copper".

Measured 2026-10-29 (doc 05 §63, `bench/records/m22-endgame`): items 1 and 2 are built (`--rescue`, `--endgame`), as is part of item 4
(`--plane-informed`). All are off. On the ten failing boards at 20 M work, the end-game with rescue routes 10 more connections than the base
(7,185 against 7,175). The same extra budget spent on plain routing routes 18 more, and rescue in the main loop routes 126 fewer. The
study's 139 connections that "route alone" came from time-limited runs. The gate is not met by these items.

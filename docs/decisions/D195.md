# D195 — Repair windows along the connection's bare-board route, focused attempts

## Context

The chain measurement (doc 20 §2.9) found that a connection a rip-up could close is closed most often by routing it together with the
nets that block it, and that the router's own list of blockers was never enough: the nets next to the route the connection takes on a
board without the other nets' copper were. `route --repair` (D172) already lifts copper and routes again, but looks for it round the two
ends of the connection, and each attempt routes everything that is open.

## Decision

Three options of the repair stage (`src/passes/repair.{hpp,cpp}`, doc 20 §2.11), all off by default:

- `--repair-select route|both` (default `ends`): the window follows the route the connection takes with every other net's unlocked copper
  off the board (one more routing run per connection), on the layers that route uses, at 0.2 and 1 mm (`--repair-route-mm`). A connection
  with no such route is counted (`no_bare_route`) and left alone. `both`: along the route, then the end windows.
- `--repair-focus`: an attempt routes only the connection's net and the nets it lifted copper of. The open connections of the other nets
  keep their place in the list.
- `--repair-variants N`: each attempt runs N portfolio variants and takes the best.

An attempt is kept as before: only when no joined pads come apart and fewer connections are open (rule 4).

## Alternatives

- A separate last stage built from `tracemaker chains`: the repair stage already has the lifting by branches, the keep rule and the
  bookkeeping of the input's copper; a second stage would repeat them.
- The held-first chain to depth 4 of the measurement: it closed 24 of 120 at up to dozens of runs each; not built into the router.

## Consequences

`bench/records/finish-2026-11`. After a 20 M run on the 33 failing PCBench boards the end windows close 28 of 887, the route selection
30 at 39 % less work, both 47; no board completes. Three times the budget from nothing closes 159 and completes 3 more, so the repair
alone is not worth its work on a board routed from nothing. Its place is inside `route --finish` (D196), where few connections are open.
Unfocused attempts fail on a board with many open connections (d20, 129 open: 126 attempts, each ended with more open); focused,
CoreOne goes 14 → 11 and EtherCAT 20 → 15 where unfocused attempts kept nothing. `--repair-variants 8` completed LeeChee (1 → 0) and
closed 1 of PCIE's 2; four other boards one short stayed. The focused repair was not measured on all 33 boards (the runs were stopped).

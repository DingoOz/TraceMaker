# D173 — Tuned nets stay tuned: the clean-up tunes the best state, after-route stages keep length and skew

## Context

Issue #60: `tests/integration/delay_room.py` failed on main after D154 (PR #59). The router's log said `/Debugger/D+` was tuned to
78.44 ps, but the DRC of the written board measured it at 70.56 ps against D-'s 79.25 ps, with a 0.5 ps skew rule. The issue suspected
a stage after tuning (the soft-zone refill repair or the clean-up). Debug output after each stage of `app/route_job.cpp` showed the
pair's copper unchanged from the router's result to the written board: the router itself returned the pair untuned. The winning
variant ended its search with 128 connections routed against a best of 129. The clean-up, which holds the tuners, runs only when the
search ends in its best state, so the best state went out as snapshotted, untuned. The tuning lines in the log came from other
variants. D154's late reachability proofs changed where this variant's search ended. Doc 05 §74; records in `bench/records/issue60/`.

## Decision

- **The clean-up tunes the state the router returns.** When the clean-up has tuning to do (`Router::wants_tuning`) and the search
  ended away from its best state, the best state goes back on the board first (`restore_best`, the end-game retry's restore of D155,
  now shared). The clean-up and the tuners then run on it. "Tuning to do" means a net with a `length` rule (mm or time), a `skew`
  rule or a pair skew constraint, `--pair-skew-mm`, a component-rule pair skew above 0, or buses. Boards without any of these keep the
  old behaviour, byte for byte.
- **The stages after routing may not undo tuning.** On a board with `length` or `skew` rules, the soft-zone refill repair, each pad's
  thermal relief repair and the stitching vias are kept only if no net is further outside such a rule with their copper than without
  it (`drc::length_faults`, `drc::length_faults_no_worse`, measured as the DRC measures the written board; `DrcOptions::lengths_only`
  runs only those checks). Widening and the tuning-profile narrowing change only widths, which the length calculation ignores.
- No option: both parts are correctness fixes for rules the board states, and neither changes a board without such rules.

## Alternatives

- **Exempt constrained nets from the stages after routing** (never rip or extend them): this would not have fixed issue #60, which
  had no such stage involved. It would also stop the repairs from completing a constrained net that the router left open.
- **Run the clean-up on the best state of every board:** this is the general form, and it would also optimise vias on boards without
  rules. It changes results everywhere, so it needs a tier measurement of its own. Left open.
- **Re-tune after every stage in `route_job`:** the tuners live in the router, with its obstacle model and work budget. Running a
  router again only to tune would cost another router build per stage. A stage that makes a net worse is rejected instead.

## Consequences

- `delay_room` passes again: 129/147 routed, as on main, with no skew violation (main: 1).
- The B2 fixtures before and after are in `bench/records/issue60/README.md`.
- The quick tier and other boards without length or skew rules are unchanged (two quick boards checked byte-identical; none of the 30
  has a `.kicad_dru`).
- No CI test makes the router's search end away from its best state on a tuned net. `delay_room` covers that where `build/demos` is
  prepared. The guard has a CI unit test on KiCad's `time_calculations` board.

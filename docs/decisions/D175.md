# D175 — Coupled pairs: leg reach, protected halves, a re-coupling allowance and pad exits

## Context

Issue #67, reported by quanln (GitHub @quanln): on a small 2-layer board (298 connections, 6 differential pairs) routed with
`--diff-pairs`, the two long pairs ended 0-3 % coupled where the hand-routed board has 88-100 %. quanln found three causes and
wrote two patches in their fork (not public), `--pair-leg-mm` and `--protect-coupled`; the first two options here are our own
implementation of their design, credited to them.

1. The coupled section must start within at most 2.5 mm of an end's pad midpoint (`reach` in `route_pair`). Where pads of other
   nets stand between and beside the pair's pads, no leg that short gets round them.
2. Negotiation rips one coupled half, `rip()` takes the other with it (D50), and the clean-up re-coupling (`route_diff_pairs(true)`)
   runs only on budget left over, which a run that spends its whole budget (every `--time` run, most `--work` runs) never has.
3. End legs into 0402 pads flanked by GND pads: 82,450 goal candidates, none legal. Not solved in the report.

Doc 05 §73; records in `bench/records/issue67/`.

## Decision

- `--pair-leg-mm X` (`RouterOptions::pair_leg_max`, default 2.5 mm, the old clamp): the longest reach of the uncoupled legs. The
  pair search's window grows with it (3 mm, or the reach plus 0.5 mm, plus a quarter of the pair's length).
- `--protect-coupled` (`protect_coupled`, off): a negotiated route that would rip a half of a coupled pair fails instead, with
  "would rip a coupled pair half" (reason code `rip_limit`), next to the bus and bundle-lane checks.
- `--recouple-work N` (`recouple_work`, **default 2,000,000**): the clean-up re-coupling may spend N work units past the search
  budget. Under a time limit alone the allowance becomes the time that much work takes at the run's average rate so far
  (`allowance_scale`). It is the room tuner's mechanism (D152); routing gives nothing up for it. `0` restores the old behaviour.
- `--pair-pad-exits` (`pair_pad_exits`, off): where no direct leg of a candidate is legal, the same leg shapes after a straight exit
  from the pad centre along one of 8 directions, out to r + w, 2r + w or 4r + w (r half the pad's larger side, w the leg width). The
  legal exits of a pad are found once per pair attempt (the board does not change during one); every exit check is a work unit (D77).
- `--pair-retry-leg-mm X` (`pair_retry_leg`, 0 = off): an attempt that ends with "no coupled path" is tried again with legs of up to
  X, then with pad exits at the configured reach. A pair the first attempt couples is routed exactly as without it.
  `RouteResult::pair_retried` counts the pairs the retry coupled.

Only `--recouple-work` changes a default, and only for runs that route pairs (`--diff-pairs`, component-rule pairs). With
`--recouple-work 0` and the rest at their defaults the output is byte-identical to the router before (20 boards checked).

## Alternatives

- A longer reach by default (4 or 6 mm): fewer pairs coupled on the 18 PCBench pair boards (≥ 80 %: 19 → 15 / 11), since the search
  accepts the first legal goal candidate and a far one comes up first. The retry is the form that cannot hurt a pair that couples
  today; on the public boards it coupled no extra pair, so it is not on by default either.
- Protecting coupled halves by default: 3 connections and 2 clean boards fewer on the same boards, and less coupling on balance.
- Reserving a share of the budget for re-coupling: tried in M12 and removed (doc 05 §15), because on a board that uses its whole
  budget the share is taken from routing. The allowance past the budget takes nothing from routing.
- Exit legs mixed with the direct legs by length (the first version): the cheaper exit legs displaced legal direct ones and cost
  coupling and connections; exits are now tried only where no direct leg is legal.
- A full model of a pad's legal entry directions (from its shape, its neighbours and the clearance), or end legs that start
  uncoupled and couple after the obstacle: not built; the exits and the long-leg retry are the simple forms of each.

## Consequences

- On the 18 PCBench pair boards at 30 M work the new default couples two more pairs (PmodHDMIIn 4 → 29 %, USB-C-Screen-Adapter L2
  0 → 89 %) and changes nothing else; 18 of 20 boards byte-identical; completion and KiCad DRC equal. Runs that route pairs may run
  up to 2 M work units longer in the clean-up.
- The other options are measured trade-offs, off by default; quanln's board, where (1) and (2) gave CHG_D 0 → 90.4 %, is not public,
  so their numbers are theirs.
- Cause 3 is not shown solved on a real board: pad exits couple the unit test's walled-in pads, but whether they reach quanln's
  USB_D_MCU (closest candidate 0.027 mm from legal) is not known.
- Docs: doc 05 §15 (legs, negotiation) and §73; CLI reference; `bench/pair_eval.py` gained `--mode`, `--tm` and `--time`
  (a 3600 s safety limit, so the work budget ends each run; before, the default 120 s ended some runs on a loaded machine).

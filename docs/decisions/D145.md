# D145 — KiCad 10 tuning profiles: KiCad's width rules, and per-layer widths by narrowing

## Context

Doc 18 B1 (M21): KiCad 10 projects can give a net class a tuning profile, with a track width and pair gap per signal layer, and KiCad's
DRC then requires those widths when the project sets `tuning_profile_track_geometries` to a severity other than `ignore`. The engine
read none of it, and its router has one width per net (doc 15 §15). The format and KiCad's rule were taken from KiCad 10.0's sources
(`common/project/tuning_profiles.cpp`, `pcbnew/drc/drc_engine.cpp`) and its QA projects, and checked against `kicad-cli` 10.0.6.
Design: doc 05 §58. Records: `bench/records/m21-b1/`.

## Decision

- `read_design_rules` reads `tuning_profiles` and each class's `tuning_profile` (`model::TuningProfile`, `DesignRules::profile_of`).
  It adds KiCad's implicit rules (`CustomRule::implicit`) in KiCad's place, before the custom rules, carrying the severity of
  `tuning_profile_track_geometries`, `ignore` included:
  - per profiled layer, an exact `track_width`, with `A.inDiffPair('*')` added for a differential profile;
  - for pairs, an exact `diff_pair_gap`.

  The rules have KiCad's name ("tuning profile 'NAME'"), so the engine's DRC and KiCad's report the same violation.
- Where KiCad 10.0.6's behaviour differs from its source, the engine follows the behaviour (doc 05 §58):
  - The severity of `track_width` comes from the last matching rule, so an ignored profile rule hides the board minimum.
  - `hasExactNetclass` is evaluated only in implicit rules; in a user's rule KiCad never matches it.
  - The coupled-pair clearance at the profile's gap applies to the two halves' tracks only (`NetClass::tuning_pair_gap`), not to a
    track against the partner's pads. KiCad's `AB.isCoupledDiffPair()` clearance rule is not added.
- Routing, whatever the severity (a profile is the designer's statement of the impedance):
  - A single-ended profile's nets route at the widest of its widths. After routing, each track is narrowed to its layer's width,
    judged like the other after-route passes.
  - A differential profile's pairs route at one layer's width and gap, preferring a layer the class's SMD pads are on, then the
    geometry most layers share. A `disallow track` rule keeps them off layers with another width or gap.
- Width and pair-gap rules do not switch the router to exact checks (they set no clearance).
- `--json` has `tuning_profiles`, and the log has a line per profile; a class naming an undefined profile is warned about.
- The delays (per layer, per via, time-domain tuning) are read but not used: they are B2's.

## Alternatives

- **Per-layer width inside the router.** About twenty call sites of the router take one width per net (clearance disks, via codes,
  escape stubs, caches keyed by width); changing all of them is a large, risky change for a gain that narrowing gives for single nets.
- **Route at the most common width, and keep other layers clear (also for single-ended nets).** Correct, but it gives up layers. On
  boards whose inner layers are narrower, the nets would lose the inner layers entirely.
- **Narrow pairs too.** A pair's centrelines stay where they are, so narrowing widens its gap by the width removed; KiCad's exact gap
  rule would then fail on those layers.
- **Apply the profile only when KiCad would check it.** Then the router's tracks would differ from the designer's stated
  impedance just because the check is off; the widths are the design either way.
- **Follow KiCad's source rather than its behaviour** (evaluate `hasExactNetclass` everywhere, relax the pads to the profile's gap).
  The judge is `kicad-cli`: on RoyalBlue that gave three added `clearance` errors, and the parity corpus would disagree on two
  cases.

## Consequences

- Classes without a profile, and projects without profiles, are unchanged: the reader adds nothing and the route job does nothing.
- Pairs with a profile route on fewer layers when the profile's layers disagree.
- Single-ended nets keep the clearance of their widest layer on every layer. That is conservative on the narrower layers.
- Measured (`bench/records/m21-b1`, KiCad 10.0.6 with the check at `error`): no added KiCad error on two plane-tier boards given
  profiles. On RoyalBlue, 68 inner tracks are narrowed, and routing with the single-ended profile alone is the same as without
  profiles (125/147). The USB pair profile costs five connections (120/147).
- The parity corpus has four new cases, and all 220 match KiCad.
- No real board with profiles was available; the Kookabus boards are still missing (doc 18 §7).
- Found afterwards (B2, same night; D147): the fixtures' net settings were at schema 4, which KiCad migrates by giving every class an
  empty profile, so the "no added KiCad error" above was measured by a KiCad that checked no profile. Rejudged at schema 5, three fixes
  followed (Default's profile inherited, the pair width from a single-ended profile, no neck-down on profiled nets). Results with those
  fixes: plane_smd 48/49 with no added error; RoyalBlue 102/147 with the single-ended profile and 104/147 with both. Both have no width
  error, and the second has 5 `diff_pair_gap_out_of_range` errors at the USB-C connector. "Narrowing is free" does not hold (doc 05 §58,
  `bench/records/m21-b1/runs/b1-v5`).

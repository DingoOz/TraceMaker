# D190 — Placement closure: a bandit over kinds of placement change until the board routes

## Context

The request was a feature that, when the router cannot route a board completely, moves components until it can. It
should learn whether a slight or a radical change is needed. The placer already had the pieces, run with fixed
recipes:

- `eco` (doc 04 §4): small moves next to the failed connections, a fixed number per round.
- `routable` (§3 G, §8): seeds, penalty rounds, then ECO.

Doc 04 §8.4 listed the bandit over ECO move types and side flips in ECO as not done. Earlier measurements guide the
design:

- On the M8 development set, `eco` closed 3 of 7 incomplete boards with 1–2 moved parts (§8.3).
- On 8 hard boards no placement arm closed any (placement closure experiment, 2026-10-16). Their open connections
  were pins boxed in by dense packages, and a larger route budget did as well as placement.

## Decision

`tracemaker-place --mode closure` (doc 04 §11).

- Route the input. If it is complete, write it unchanged.
- Otherwise run a UCB1 bandit (Auer et al. 2002) over eight kinds of change: shift, rotate, swap, flip (only with
  `--flip`), room (push every neighbour of a failed pad away at once), far shift, window re-place, and whole-board
  re-place.
  - Each pull is one changed placement, routed with the check budget, and kept only if fewer connections stay
    unrouted.
  - The value is the prior-smoothed success rate divided by the arm's cost in routes, plus an exploration bonus.
  - After `--closure-stall` (6) trials without a win, the next trial is radical (window or replace).
- `--closure-routes` (40) bounds the trials and `--loop-time` stops the job.
- `--closure-learn FILE` keeps the arms' trials and wins across runs as the next run's prior (capped at 4
  pseudo-trials per arm).
- A moved part must have no conflict at all, as the placer's legality and KiCad judge it. Overlaps the input already
  has stay, but a part in such an overlap can only move to a free spot.
- The final verification route of §8.3b applies as in `routable` and `eco`.

## Alternatives

- **ALNS roulette weights** (Ropke and Pisinger 2006). They adapt per segment and need far more iterations than the
  20–40 routes a board gets. UCB with a prior decides from the first trials.
- **Learned placement** (RL, a GNN routability predictor). It needs training data per router, and its output still
  has to be routed to be trusted. Not now.
- **More routability-driven global placement** (RUDY, cell inflation). This exists as `routable`'s seeds. It
  predicts congestion, not this router's failures; the loop uses it as its radical arm.
- **Tolerating a moved part's existing overlaps** (first version). It made more moves possible on hand placements
  that have conflicts in the model, but KiCad reported the changed overlaps as new errors (1Bitsy: 2 courtyard, 1
  short, 2 clearance, 3 mask bridge). Rejected; a unit test now guards it.

## Consequences

Measured in `bench/records/m24-closure` (doc 04 §11, 14 boards incomplete with the human placement):

- **At 10 M:** open connections 58 → 32, 4 boards complete, 3 clean, none worse.
- **Against a 4× route budget on the human placement** (40 open, 3 clean): the closure placements routed at the same
  40 M leave 30 open and give 5 complete and 4 clean boards. ardfpga is one worse (4 → 5).
- **Kept changes were mostly 0.5–1 mm shifts, rotations and swaps.** Whole-board re-placement never won.
- **Cost:** about 25 minutes per board at the median (10–15 routes).

Not built:

- Rerouting only the nets next to a change (doc 04 §4 step 3).
- Exact courtyard polygons in the legality model. Three boards had no legal move because their hand placements
  overlap in the conservative model.
- Moves proposed from the router's cut geometry (`PlacementRequest`).

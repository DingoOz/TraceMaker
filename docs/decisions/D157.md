# D157 — Fan-out heads: the designer's escapes laid before routing

## Context

M22 item 8 (the via-site and layer planner) was developed on one board, `mini_mass_prog_bench_prog_rig`, after D156 left it at 987
of 995. The open pins are on U2, a 0.5 mm QFN-44, boxed in by their neighbours' tracks and vias in a ring 1-2 mm out.

An oracle experiment (records: [bench/records/m22-oneboard](../../bench/records/m22-oneboard/README.md), [doc 05 §65](../05-routing.md))
put the designer's own copper near U2 into the unrouted board as fixed copper:

- All of it within 5 mm routed 969 of 973. That copper already completes 22 local connections, so the count is not comparable.
- Only the parts that touch a single pad (the escape heads) routed 990 of 995. The base routes 986.
- Only the parts that join two or more pads (the local connections) routed 967 of 973.
- Straight 5 mm stubs from every pin did worse: 978 of 989 and 983 of 993.

So what helps is the shape of the escapes. The designer keeps each side of U2 either straight or in staggered 45-degree bundles toward
the pins' destinations. A side stays straight where its bundles would run into the parts beside the package.

## Decision

`--fanout-heads MM` (off by default; `--fanout-heads-min-pins`, 24) lays a head on the pad's layer before the router builds its
obstacles. It applies to each perimeter pin of a package with pins on all four sides.

- **Which pins.** A pin gets no head when its net has a zone, or no pad off the package. It gets none either when its nearest such pad
  lies within MM of the package, since local connections are the router's.
- **Shape.** Along a side the pins split into three blocks, as many as possible turning the way their destination asks:
  - a block turning toward the low end,
  - a straight block,
  - a block turning toward the high end.

  A pin turns when its destination is more than 30 degrees off the side's normal. Turning pins bend 0.3 mm past the pad, each one
  0.1 mm further out than its neighbour toward the corner. That is ordered escape with one bend (Kong, Yan and Wong, ASP-DAC 2010),
  and it keeps parallel diagonals track plus clearance apart.
- **Turn or straight.** Each side is laid both ways against what is already there, and keeps the one with more head length.
- **Checks.** Each leg is cut where the exact check refuses it. A cut head ends 0.5 mm short of the obstacle, so its own route has room
  to leave.
- **Joins nothing.** A head keeps clearance from every other pad and piece of copper of its net, so it joins nothing but its own pad.
  The connection plan does not change.
- **During and after routing.** The heads are fixed copper of their net in the router's copy of the board. Other nets route round
  them, and the pin's own route runs over them. They are not routed copper, so they are in no result and are not written out. Each
  route runs pad to pad, so leaving them out disconnects nothing (checked: the KiCad DRC of the output has no added unconnected items).
- **Debugging.** `TM_DEBUG_HEADS` prints each head.

Code: `src/route/fanout_heads.{hpp,cpp}`, `Router::place_fanout_heads`.

## Alternatives

- Placeholder holds along the straight corridors, released when the pin routes and after the strict pass (`--fanout-hold`, D156):
  979-987 on mini_mass.
- Straight stubs from every pin, injected as fixed copper: 978 and 983 of about 990.
- Always bending toward the destination: 983 and 984. Bending only beyond 60 degrees: 986 and 987. On this board the destinations of
  the top pins are more sideways than those of the left pins, yet the designer keeps the top straight. Space decides, not angle.
- Heads cut from the router's own routes of U2's nets routed first. The first routes alone boxed in five U2 pins, so this was not run
  to the end.
- Real partial routes (a connection that starts from its head's end). That would be a larger change to the router's state, and it is
  not needed: the pin's own route already runs over its head.

## Consequences

On mini_mass (variant 6, 20 M work):

| Arm | Routed |
|---|--:|
| base | 986 |
| `--via-shove` (D156) | 987 |
| `--fanout-heads 3` | 988 |
| `--fanout-heads 5` | 989 |
| `--fanout-heads 5 --via-shove` | 989 |
| the designer's heads (oracle) | 990 |

- The output adds no KiCad DRC errors.
- Left open: RESET (U2.13), USB_D- (U2.3), +5V C1-R6, and the three long INT nets. The INT nets fail on the search budget, not on
  escape.
- On the ten failing boards (8 variants, 20 M work), `--fanout-heads 5` routes 7,175, the same as the base. Against it, the base at 22 M
  routes 7,193 and `--via-shove --escape-all` 7,197. Of the six boards that get heads, mini_mass and LeeChee gain 3 each, teensy is
  unchanged, and EtherCAT, poncho and CoreOne lose 1-3. With `--via-shove --escape-all` added it routes 7,190.
- The rule for turning or keeping a side straight fits mini_mass's U2 and does not carry over to the other boards' packages. The
  option stays off.
- On CoreOne the designer's own single-pad copper round U801 routes 785 of 806, against 787 for the base (our heads on U801 alone:
  786). Most of those escapes end at a via, and even they do not help, so a better turning rule was not built.
- With the options off the quick tier is byte-identical (30 boards). No board gained DRC errors.
- Docs changed: doc 05 §65, doc 18 §18.

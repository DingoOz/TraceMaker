# D161 — Why a connection is open: an escape proof for the pins of open connections

## Context

Doc 20 §2.4, step 4 of the plan for 100 % routed boards. A run that leaves connections open did not say whether any router could
connect them. The router's `boxed_in` reason means its own searches found no way out, which includes the routed copper of other nets.
On the 33 failing PCBench tier B-D boards it was the most common reason (295 of 727 open connections). The route skill told agents it
was a placement problem that budget cannot fix. A user needs the pins that cannot be connected as placed, with what walls them in, told
apart from the congested ones.

## Decision

- **An escape proof per pin** (`analysis/escape_proof.{hpp,cpp}`, doc 05 §69). The board's fixed copper of other nets is grown by each
  pair's clearance plus the track's half width, rasterised on a 10 µm grid 1.5 mm beyond the pin, and flood-filled from the pin on each
  of its layers, with via sites checked on all layers. Cells count as free within half a cell diagonal, so "walled in" is a proof.
- **Judged at the board's minimums.** A pin walled in for its class's track and via is checked again with the minimum track width and
  via. Only a pin walled in at the minimums is `enclosed` (proved). The others are reported as needing a narrower track or via, as
  walled in only by zone fills or net-less copper graphics on the pin (naming `--soft-zones` or `--soft-copper-graphics`), or as
  congested.
- **On by default in `tracemaker route`**, after the run, for the end pins of the open connections (at most 400). It writes log lines
  and the summary's `why_open`; `--no-why-open` turns it off. It does not change the routing.
- **`tracemaker walled BOARD [--pin REF.NUM] [--json]`** runs the same check before routing.
- **Next steps, offered** (doc 20 §2.5). The run ends with "next:" lines (`why_open.next`) by cause. Pin swaps, placement nudges and
  more layers change the design and gained nothing measurable as silent rungs (swaps 5 better and 5 worse; placements made no board
  clean). They are named, not run.

## Alternatives

- **The cut test on straight lines** (doc 20 §2.4 as first written, and `--cut-report`). It found no over-full board-scale cut on tiers
  B-D. A pad-level cut names a gap but not whether another way round exists; the flood fill decides that.
- **Judging at the class's track width.** It raised six false alarms on a clean board (wavegen's +3V3 at 1.02 mm, necked down by the
  router). A class's width is a default, not a DRC limit.
- **Inside the router's search.** It is already there as `boxed_in`, mixed with congestion. The proof must see only what no router can
  move.

## Consequences

- No false alarm on the inputs of the 405 KiCad-clean benchmark boards (41,810 pins).
- On the 33 failing boards (1,341 pins of 727 open connections): none is walled in by the placement, 8 only for their class's track, and
  14 only by E3's net-less copper pours, which `--soft-copper-graphics` frees. The other 1,319 are congestion. The skill's advice on
  `boxed_in` was wrong for these boards and is corrected.
- About 19 ms per pin on one thread, after routing only.
- The proof is necessary-condition only: it cannot show that a pin which gets out past fixed copper will be routed. Custom rules that
  raise a net's minimum width are not used, so the proof is weaker than it could be there, never wrong.
- Records: `bench/records/complete-routing/escape`. Test: `tests/test_escape_proof.cpp`. Docs: doc 05 §69, doc 20 §2.4 and §4, README,
  the route skill.

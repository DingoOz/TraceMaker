# D156 — Last-mile repairs developed on one board: via shove, fan-out hold, negotiation bounds

## Context

The repair steps of D155 (pin rescue, end-game retry) lost to an equal budget on ten failing boards. The user changed the method:
develop each M22 change on one board that no earlier run completed (`mini_mass_prog_bench_prog_rig`, 986 of 995 at 20 M work in
every arm), and run the other failing boards only once M22 is developed on it. Traces of that board showed three things
([records](../../bench/records/m22-oneboard/README.md), [doc 05 §64](../05-routing.md)):

- U2's stuck pins are boxed in by their neighbours' fan-out copper.
- Negotiation spends 25 M of its 30 M work on strict retries that fail, before the negotiated searches, which succeed cheaply.
- A negotiation pass whose successes rip long nets never ends, so its crossing cost never rises.

The user also asked whether the router moves nearby vias to open a path. It did not: rip-up removes whole connections.

## Decision

New options, all off by default. With them off the output is byte-identical (quick tier, 30 boards).

- `--via-shove` (M22 item 7). A boxed-in connection lifts the nearest vias of other nets with only the tracks ending at them: within
  `--via-shove-mm` (1.5 mm), up to 6 vias, then pairs of the nearest 4. It is then searched once without them.
  - When it routes, each via goes down at the nearest position where it and its re-drawn legs pass the exact check (6 rings of 16,
    0.15 mm apart).
  - Failing that, the via is put at each position in turn and the connection searched round it.
  - Otherwise everything is put back.

  It does at most 16 searches per connection, 300 k expansions each, and spends at most 5 % of the run's work budget
  (`--via-shove-share`).
- `--fanout-hold`: through the strict first pass, a placeholder track of its net along each open pin's straight escape corridor.
- Three negotiation options:
  - `--neg-strict-cap N` caps the strict searches of negotiation passes.
  - `--long-weight W` (with `--long-weight-mm`, 100) makes the negotiated searches of long connections weighted A*.
  - `--pass-bound` limits each negotiation pass to the connections queued at its start.
- `--window-escalate` (item 3), `--via-retry` (item 5, D153, only after a cap miss), and `--escape-all` and `--pin-via-pen` (item 6).
- `TM_PICK_VARIANTS` (debugging): run only the listed portfolio variants.

## Alternatives

- Track shove (moving other nets' tracks). The search's endpoints are pads, so re-joining a cut route needs point endpoints in the
  search and the commit. Pin rescue already showed that re-routing the neighbour boxes the neighbour in. Left for the via-site
  planner (item 8).
- More budget: 40 M and 60 M route exactly 986 on mini_mass; the extra goes to whole-board searches that fail at their cap.
- The global router's corridors: 975 and 971 on mini_mass.

## Consequences

On ten failing boards at 20 M work (8 variants):

- `--via-shove --escape-all` routes 7,197, against 7,175 for the base and 7,193 for the base at 22 M. Open connections go from 624 to
  602, 3.5 % fewer (the M22 gate asks for 15 %).
- `--via-shove` alone routes 7,176.
- No board gained DRC errors. The gains are small next to per-board noise (±7).

Three options lost and stay off:

- `--window-escalate` cost Aleste 254 connections: its whole-board windows take the budget.
- The first, unbounded shove cost kitspace_d20 31. That is why it is capped at 5 % of the budget.
- `--pin-via-pen 3` lost on two of three boards.

The negotiation options change nothing on these boards. Negotiation on mini_mass is lossy: every pass after the first routes fewer.
mini_mass ends at 987. Five U2 pins boxed in by their neighbours' tracks and the three long INT nets remain open.

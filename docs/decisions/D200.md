# D200 — Rip-up chains are applied: chains --apply and a ladder rung

## Context

`tracemaker chains` (doc 20 §2.9, D194) measures, for each open connection of a routed board, which nets must be lifted for it and whether
they route again. It only measured: a chain that closed was thrown away. After the stronger ladder eight boards were 1 to 6 short, and
their open connections were not helped by a finer lattice (half the pitch on four of them: no change). The measurement on the same four
said one of MonApollo's one and one of OtterPill's two close with a chain of depth 3 and 2.

## Decision

- `tracemaker chains BOARD --apply OUT` closes the open connections one after the other, each on the board the ones before left, and
  writes the result. It is written only when fewer connections are open than on the input (counted on the board as written). Copper a
  chain held for its own deeper levels is free again for the next chain; the file's locked copper is never lifted. Pieces left with no
  pad are taken off (D198). Without `--apply` the measurement is as before (every connection on the same board, in parallel).
- `route --finish --finish-chains` adds the same as a rung before the first added layer and again after the last. A rung is kept only when
  it leaves fewer open, like every other.

Off by default.

## Alternatives

- *Chains inside the router's loop.* The router already rips and re-routes under negotiation; the chain differs in holding the new
  connection and following each displaced net in turn, with a full run per step. As a rung at the end it costs nothing on boards that
  are complete.
- *Backtracking over the order of the chains.* Not built: the measurement has none either, and the boards in question have 1 to 6 open.

## Consequences

- MonApollo (1 open after the stronger ladder): `route --finish --finish-chains` on that board ends with 0 open, KiCad 0 unconnected.
  OtterPill 2 -> 1 (the other has no path on the bare board).
- Test: `[chains]` (the applied board has nothing open and routes to nothing open again).
- Open: a chain rung runs the router many times (49 runs for MonApollo's one connection); it is serial under `--apply`.

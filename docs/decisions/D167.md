# D167 — physical_clearance between copper of any net in the router and the DRC

## Context

Issue #64: a `.kicad_dru` rule `(constraint physical_clearance (min 0.1mm)) (condition "A.Type == 'Via' && B.Type == 'Pad'")` (no
via in a pad) was not applied by the router or by `tracemaker drc`. The only message came from the zone refill, after routing. On
the reporter's relaymini board the routed output had 25 violations in KiCad's DRC, all vias in pads of their own net. The
net-class clearance does not hold within a net, so nothing kept them out.

KiCad's provider (`drc_test_provider_physical_clearance.cpp`, 10.0) tests every pair of items on a common layer with no net test.
The last matching rule decides, a rule with severity `ignore` tests nothing, and a hit is reported as `clearance` once per pair.
So a rule that matches a track and its own pad is broken by every connection.

## Decision

- **Rule engine:** `physical_clearance(a, b, layer)` returns the last matching rule's minimum, or -1 when none matches or that
  rule is ignored. `via_rule_hits_own_tracks` (renamed from `via_hole_rule_hits_own_tracks`) also holds for a physical_clearance
  rule between a net's vias and its own tracks, so such a net gets no vias, as with `physical_hole_clearance` (D72).
- **Router:**
  - `Obstacles::physical_state` tests new tracks and vias against fixed and routed copper in the exact check before commit and in
    the routed checks.
  - `physical_codes` gives the per-class caches either a block, or, for a pair with a track, the item's net.
  - A pair with a track of the probe's own net is left out, because a track meets the copper of its net wherever it connects. The
    rule engine warns, by rule name, when a rule matches a track and a pad or track of one net.
  - Vias against pads and vias of their own net are tested; that is the issue's case.
  - A condition that names nets turns the caches off (`needs_exact_routing`), as for the other physical rule (D139).
- **DRC:** `check_physical_clearance` reports `clearance` with the rule's name and the actual gap. It reports once per pair, and an
  item against a zone fill once per zone and layer. The severity comes from the rule.
- **Zone refill:** still does not keep the rule. Its warning now says the rule is not applied to zone fills only.

## Alternatives

- **Only warn before routing** (the issue's second ask). The rule is cheap to keep where it matters, and a warning still leaves a
  board that fails KiCad's DRC.
- **Keep the rule between a track and its own pad too, as KiCad reads it literally.** No connection could then be routed under
  such a rule. Leaving those pairs out, and naming the rule in a warning, routes the board and keeps the rule everywhere else.

## Consequences

- relaymini with `via_not_in_pad` at 3 M work, 2 variants: 0 `via_not_in_pad` violations in KiCad's DRC, against 25 before.
  Routed 57 of 72 against 60: vias may no longer sit in or near pads. The other violations are the input board's own, unchanged.
  `tracemaker drc` gives the same counts as KiCad on both outputs (25 and 0; 40 and 15 `clearance` in all).
- Boards without a physical_clearance rule are unaffected: every new check returns at once.
- Tests in `tests/test_design_rules.cpp`; docs: doc 05 §27, doc 08, and the board-prep skill's rule table and preflight.

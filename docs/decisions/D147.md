# D147 — Time-domain matching measured as KiCad 10.0.6 measures it, and KiCad's net-class and pair-coupling semantics

## Context

Doc 18 B2 (M21): length and skew groups in picoseconds, with per-layer and via delays from B1's tuning profiles, tuned to a target
delay and checked against KiCad's own rules. The gate is a DDR-style generated fixture and two real boards within tolerance by KiCad's
DRC, in delay. The engine read ps as a unit but measured lengths its own way, and its router read time rules as lengths. Checking B2
against KiCad also showed that the B1 fixtures (D145) had been judged by a KiCad that read no profile at all (net settings at schema 4).
Design: doc 05 §59 and the note at the end of §58. Records: `bench/records/m21-b2` and `bench/records/m21-b1/runs/b1-v5`.

## Decision

- **Measure as KiCad 10.0.6 does**, quirks included (`drc/length.hpp`): its chain merge, which depends on order on branched nets (file
  order here, pointer order in KiCad); via spans; clipping in vias and pads; and its rounding of lengths and delays. The DRC's `length`
  and `skew` check groups items by rule and compares a ps rule with the delay. A minimum skew fails on the reference net, as in 10.0.6.
- **Tune delays in the router** (`tune_delays`): ps windows first, then ps skew groups or pairs, with meanders weighted by each layer's
  delay per mm. Time rules are no longer read as lengths.
- **Net classes as 10.0.6 resolves them**:
  - Default joins a net's effective class when the matched classes leave a field unset, or when none of them has a profile and Default
    has one.
  - `hasNetclass` is evaluated.
  - A class with no profile takes Default's.
  - Net settings older than schema 5 lose their profiles, with a warning.
- **Profiled nets are not necked down**, and a single-ended profile sets the pair width too.
- **Port KiCad's diff-pair coupling test** into the DRC (`drc/coupling.hpp`, `check_diff_pairs`), so that `diff_pair_gap` and
  `diff_pair_uncoupled` rules are checked, the profile's implicit gap rule included. The router does not yet use it.
- The route summary no longer records `--view-embed`. Like `--view-token`, it gave away the machine's addresses.

## Alternatives

- **Follow KiCad master's merge** (simple two-end joints only). It is the better rule, but the judge is 10.0.6, and the two agree on
  every net that does not branch inside a via.
- **Tune the delay by length**, converting each window with one delay per mm. That is wrong as soon as a net changes layer or has a
  via, and a profile gives each layer its own delay.
- **Keep the B1 results** with a note. They were measured by a judge that saw no profile; they had to be redone.
- **Make the router avoid coupling-gap errors now.** That needs the coupling test inside the commit check for pair nets with a maximum
  gap, and the pad-to-pad joins of interleaved connectors handled. It is left open and reported.

## Consequences

- KiCad's QA board: the delays match to 0.01 ps, with the same one violation.
- Length parity: of 329 nets on four demo boards, 325 match to under 1 µm. Four branched power and mesh nets differ by up to 1.58 mm,
  because of 10.0.6's order dependence.
- ddr8, interf_u and RoyalBlue end KiCad-clean on their delay rules (20, 7 and 1 errors with the build before B2), interf_u and
  RoyalBlue at the same routed counts.
- B1 rejudged: plane_smd 48/49 with no added error. RoyalBlue drops to 102 (single-ended) and 104/147 (both profiles), from 125:
  KiCad's exact width with no neck-down is the price of a single-ended profile on Default. Five `diff_pair_gap_out_of_range` errors
  remain at RoyalBlue's USB-C connector.
- Open:
  - the router does not avoid coupling-gap errors;
  - `fromTo()` is not evaluated;
  - the bus tuner ignores ps skew;
  - a net in two classes uses the first one's parameters;
  - KiCad's pair-name suffixes with trailing digits are not paired.
- Docs: 05 §58 (rejudged) and §59, 18 §17, 11 (M21 row).

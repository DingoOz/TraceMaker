Detailed Description

The reference copper of one layer: the region under the non-zero rule (islands with their holes).

Its edges and vertices are bucketed in grids so that "is this point on copper?", "can a segment cross it?" and "which vertices are near?" cost the neighbourhood and not the whole pour (the plane of a large board has hundreds of thousands of edges, most of them antipads), and the vertices a shortest path can bend at.

The point of the region's boundary nearest to p, within reach; false if none.

Cells are searched in growing squares. Several edges can be exactly as near (a pad at the centre of a round antipad): the one that comes first in the region's edge order wins, so the answer does not depend on the order the grid is walked in.

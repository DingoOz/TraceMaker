# Report, 2026-10-30: thin tracks, real vias (D159)

Asked at about 09:50 UTC: "ok you have 8 hours to test and develop, go for it!", after the plan for an "always 100 %" router and the
idea of routing with impossibly thin tracks and thickening them afterwards. Everything is on the local branch `thin-vias` in the worktree
`../TraceMaker-thin`, from `origin/main` `33643ee`. Nothing is pushed. Design: doc 05 §67, D159. Records: `bench/records/d159-thin-cut`.

## What was built

| Item | What it does | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `tracemaker cuts BOARD` (`route/cut_test.{hpp,cpp}`) | Leiserson-Maley cut condition on every copper layer of a routed board: over-full cuts between pads and vias, the clearance scale s\* the topology allows, and vias with no room anywhere in their cell (a proof). About 0.3 s per board | a command |
| Exact pads in the legaliser (`route/legalize.cpp`) | Rotated pads are rotated rectangles, and ovals and rounded rectangles have their corner radius (they were bounding boxes of sharp rectangles). Offset pads are centred on their copper. A vertex whose track end was pushed off its own pad's copper goes back | **on** (only `--relax` and `--legalize` use the legaliser) |
| `--ramp-vias-real` (`DesignRules::via_clearance_extra`) | Relaxed ramp stages lower the clearance of tracks only | off |
| `--ramp-cut-prune` | Before legalising, drop routes the cut test proves cannot be legalised (vias with no room; a greedy cover of over-full pad-to-pad cuts) | off |
| Tests | `[cuts]` (3 cases: a via fits a ball-grid cell, a via with a squeezed track has no room, two tracks over-fill a gap and a dipping loop cancels); `[legalize]` rotated pad and oval | |

## What was found

1. **The cut test has no false alarms.** 405 KiCad-clean boards (tiers A-D, srj19/srj20 records), 423,870 cuts: no over-full cut with a
   wire, no via without room.
2. **On srj20 the thin sketch fails only at vias.** On the six boards complete at 0.06 mm, every wire fits between pads at 0.10 mm. Four
   vias (048 x2, 072 x2) provably have no room: a ball-grid cell's diagonal is 0.622 mm, and a via and a track need 0.72 mm. Both
   legalisers get stuck at exactly those vias. Neither option helps there: 048 72/74 and 072 75/81 in all arms, and vias-real 74/81 on
   072. Real-clearance vias make stage 1 route fewer.
3. **On PCBench relax lost because of the legaliser's pads, not the topology.** The cut test found the relaxed topologies had room (s\*
   1.17-1.76), yet a third of the routes were dropped at each hand-over. The legaliser saw rotated/oval/rounded pads as bounding boxes. On
   d20_tri, the first hand-over went from 274 of 427 routes kept to 427 of 427.
4. **33 failing PCBench tier B-D boards** (100 M work, 8 variants, KiCad-judged; base = today's main):

   | Arm | Routed (of 16,935) | vs base | Better / worse | KiCad-clean |
   |---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
   | base (main, no relax) | 16,166 | | | 2 |
   | `--relax`, bounding-box pads (main) | 15,683 | −483 | 8 / 16 | 3 |
   | `--relax`, exact pads | 15,925 | −241 | 8 / 15 | 5 |
   | `--relax`, exact pads, subdivide 0.1 | 15,955 | −211 | 10 / 13 | 5 |

   - Relax with exact pads completes serial_gw, teensy-fx and ardfpga, which the base does not. The base's two clean boards stay clean.
   - The better of base and relax per board gives 16,208 (+42), or 16,214 (+48) with subdivide, and five clean boards.
   - Subdivide against none: +30, 12 boards better, 1 worse, about 10 % more CPU. On srj20's six stuck boards at the same budget it is
     neutral (404 and 404 of 453, 2 better and 2 worse), so it stays an option and `--relax` does not turn it on.
   - Relax still loses on most large boards. The final arms with the committed engine are byte-identical to these on all 33 boards.

5. **The final hand-over** (80 % stage to the board's rules) is now where relax stalls. On poncho the cut test says there is room (s\* 1.167),
   and projection leaves 71 pairs short by 0.02-0.04 mm in one dense area.
   More sweeps (3000) leave it at 360 and a 0.5 mm box gives 363: the projection is stuck, not slow.
6. **Quick tier** 30/30 byte-identical to main, gates pass. Full `ctest --preset release` passed (2 known skips: delay_room #60, fixture
   preparation). srj20 relax output byte-identical to before (axis-aligned rectangular pads).

## Things to know

- **Mistakes on the way.**
  - The first legaliser probe ran without `--time`, so the default 120 s limit cut it and the counts depended on machine load. It was
    re-run work-bounded.
  - One frozen binary was copied while it was being linked (0 bytes), so the first quick-tier and prune replays did not run. They were
    re-run.
  - My first in-pad restore reset every vertex that left its pad's copper, even by less than the track's half width. It cost 24
    connections on the 21 boards one run had finished (d20 375 -> 356) and prevented no disconnection. It now acts only when the track's
    end no longer touches the copper. The two final 33-board arms with that engine are byte-identical to the earlier ones.
  - `pgrep -f PATTERN` inside a shell whose command contains PATTERN matches that shell (twice it stopped my own command). No other
    process was hit; kills were by listed PIDs.
- **Not committed upstream; the dev site was not updated.** It reads the main checkout, which is on your `via-retry` branch with
  uncommitted work, so I did not touch `dev/progress.json` there. One devlog note is in this worktree.
- `bench/results/thin-*` hold the raw runs; the records keep `boards.jsonl`, `summary.json` and `report.md` of each.

## Next steps this suggests

- **Relax as a rescue.** Run it after a plain run on boards left with a few open connections: it completes boards the base does not
  (serial_gw, teensy-fx, ardfpga).
- **`--relax --relax-late` on the 33 boards.** A stopped run (16 boards, with the old restore) was +46 against relax (ErgoDone +27, poncho
  +19) but cleaner on fewer boards (2 against 4). Worth a full run.
- **A realizer for the final hand-over** where the cuts say there is room and projection stalls (LP with a trust region, or shortest
  homotopic paths).
- **Room-aware thin routing on ball grids.** Count via room per cell while routing at thin clearance (the cut test's via room, as a
  cost), instead of blanket real-clearance vias.

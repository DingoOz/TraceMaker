    18 — Candidate features for the roadmap (research and plan)¶

    Status: Plan

    Nothing on this page is built yet, or it is a proposal.

    Part of the TraceMaker plan. Start at ../PLAN.md. Written 2026-10-14 after M16, issues #4–#8 and #24 were closed. Update (2026-10-16): M17 was built and measured (docs/10 §11, D121, D122, bench/records/m17). Two statements below are corrected by it: PCBWorld's "679 real boards" (§2, §3, A1) are derived from PCBench, so A1 is not an independent second corpus; and the paper was read in full (§1). The rest of this note is as written on 2026-10-14. Update (2026-10-24): D3, the run report, was built (§9, D136). Update (2026-10-27): C3, pours and the layer-count advisor, was built (§10, D138). Status (2026-10-15): adopted as the roadmap's M17–M21 on the owner's answers in §7 (decision D118). Nothing here is built. The candidates keep the numbers they have in §4; §5 is the order that the answers gave. This note changes no code. Evidence tags: [R] checked in this repository (file or doc named), [W] from a web source (vendor claims are marked as self-reported), [A] my estimate or assumption.

    1. How this was made, and what it cannot tell you¶

    Read: the roadmap, the open ("not built", "still open", "next") passages of docs 04–17, the non-goals of doc 01, the morning reports, the release page, and the current repository for each gap named below. Searched the web (US-only search tool, standard mode) for KiCad 10's release notes, tuning profiles in KiCad's source documentation, the KiCad IPC API, the commercial AI layout tools, pin swapping, and 2026 papers on learned PCB routing.

    Limits, so the ranking is read with them in mind:

    • No user research. Nothing here comes from asking people what they need. The ranking follows evidence in the repository and the field, and two guesses about the audience (§7).
    • Vendor material is self-reported. Quilter, DeepPCB and Flux publish comparisons of themselves; I found no independent validation [W] and quote no vendor number.
    • PCBWorld's paper was not read in full when this note was written (only the abstract page); it was read in full on 2026-10-16 for M17, and its D3 set is derived from PCBench (docs/10 §11.1).
    • Nothing was run for this note except reading. No candidate has a measurement of its own gain yet; each carries the experiment that would give one.

    3. What the field is doing¶

    • KiCad 10 (released 2026-03-20) [W: kicad.org release post; KiCad source documentation]. Track tuning was rebuilt: time-domain constraints (delay, not only length), tuning profiles with per-layer track width and differential gap, per-layer propagation delay, via delay overrides, a target impedance computed from the stackup, assigned to net classes; a graphical custom-rule editor; PCB design blocks (reusable layout modules); pin and gate swap with forward and back annotation (unconstrained; a constrained system "may come in future versions"); jumpers (same net, no ratsnest); inner-layer footprint graphics and keep-outs; suggested fixes for DRC errors; importers for Allegro and PADS. A .kicad_pro already has a tuning_profiles block (empty in the KiCad demos in bench/data) and the rule severities missing_tuning_profile and tuning_profile_track_geometries [R]; the engine reads neither [R: no match in src/].
    • KiCad IPC API [W]: in KiCad 9 and 10 it talks to a running GUI only; headless use, plotting and export arrive with KiCad 11; KiCad 11 stops bundling Python, so plugins move to their own interpreter. Third-party reports of editing a board through create_items and push_commit match what the plugin does [R].
    • Commercial AI layout [W, self-reported]: Quilter targets complex DDR / PCIe / RF boards and returns several candidate layouts with a scorecard; DeepPCB presents learned global routing; Flux's auto-layout runs after the engineer has routed differential pairs, impedance- controlled lines and high-speed interfaces by hand. Common ground in how they sell: critical interfaces are where trust is lost, and buyers ask whether they can see why the tool did what it did and audit constraints and violations quickly.
    • Research [W: abstract only]: PCBWorld (July 2026; open environment on KiCad, 679 real boards, eight engine-checked metrics) reports that tool-using agents beat grid-action RL and open-loop LLMs, that an RL policy trained on synthetic boards approaches rule-based routers on real ones, and that learning-based methods are not yet competitive with rule-based routers. OmniRouting (August 2026) is a benchmark for LLMs and multimodal models on routing.
    • Pin swapping [W: Altium, Siemens, Cadence material]: described as a way to cut crossovers and layers, needs an up-front definition of which pins may swap, and must be passed back to the schematic. I found no independent measurement of the gain, and one trade article warns that a swap that looks harmless can break FPGA timing.

    What this says for TraceMaker: its differentiators (determinism, exact checks, transactions, KiCad as judge, the stuck report, the audit reports) fit "auditable". The field's open ground is high-speed interfaces done with proof; KiCad 10 just made the data for that (per-layer geometry and delay) a native project setting that TraceMaker does not yet read.

    Effort: S one session, M two to three, L four or more [A]. Every candidate keeps the project rules (determinism, exact check, a reference path for every accelerated path, off by default until measured, results in bench/records/).

    5. Order (after the answers of §7)¶

    The audience is hobbyists first, with high-speed work limited to the Kookabus boards; KiCad 11 support is wanted; a proposal-only pin swap is acceptable; the agent interface is wanted, with the stop rule of D2. That puts install and measurement first, then the session server that the agent interface and the KiCad assistant share, then KiCad 11 (due about February 2027 [W]), then the hobbyist wins, and the high-speed work last and only as far as the Kookabus boards need it.

    Milestone Candidates Why here
    M17 — Installable and measured A2 prebuilt releases and plugin archive; A1 PCBWorld-Bench run; C1 --relax/--txn on the PCBench B–D failures Hobbyists cannot use what they cannot install; the other two answer, cheaply, how TraceMaker does on boards nobody tuned it on, and whether a known capability is a free gain
    M18 — Session server and agent interface D2 (the agent API) on top of a long-running board session (tracemakerd, the core of D1) Asked for. One session process serves two users: an agent that routes connection by connection, and the KiCad plugin that routes the selected nets without restarting the engine. The stop rule of D2 decides whether anything beyond the interface is built
    M19 — KiCad 11 readiness A4 made a milestone: file format, DRC parity and plugin for 11 KiCad 11 is expected about February 2027 [W]: headless IPC (kicad-cli api-server), plotting and export through the API, no bundled Python. No official file-format change list was found [W], so the first task is the watch job, the second is reading a release candidate when one exists
    M20 — Hobbyist wins C3 pours and a layer-count advisor; D3 run report; C2's experiment and a proposal-only pin swap; C5's classification Two-layer and four-layer boards are the common case; the layer count is a direct cost; a report makes a run auditable
    M21 — High speed for the Kookabus boards A3 reduced to the Kookabus boards; B1 tuning profiles; B2 time-domain matching; B3 the audit loop; A5 Only as far as those boards need. The files must be found first (§7)

    C6, C7, C4 and B4 wait for a board that needs them.

    6. Considered and not recommended now¶

    • A learned or RL router. PCBWorld's authors say learning-based methods are not yet competitive with rule-based routers [W], and M12 measured that learned ordering and congestion models would learn little here (doc 05 §25) [R].
    • GPU global routing / GPU DRC broad-phase. Measured to not pay (D55, D56) [R].
    • Signal-integrity simulation. A non-goal (doc 01). The useful, cheaper form is to export what an external field solver needs (stackup, geometry), not to build one.
    • A gridless tile-plane arm. Its measured upper bound is a few connections on the boards tried (doc 05 §25) [R].
    • Rigid-flex. No demand in the repository or the sources read [A].
    • A hosted service. The determinism and "your board never leaves the machine" are strengths; nothing here argues for it [A].

    8. What could make this plan wrong¶

    • B1 leans on KiCad 10 adoption: if the Kookabus boards (and hobbyists) do not define tuning profiles, B1 matters little. A3 will show it, and M21 is last for that reason.
    • PCBWorld's boards may be mostly simple, so A1 could look good and teach little; the failure classification is the useful output, not the percentage.
    • C1 may find nothing: --relax may need the breakout structure to help. A recorded negative still removes a guess.
    • Vendor comparisons are self-reported; the pull toward "AI layout" features may be marketing rather than need.
    • All effort figures are guesses from past sessions.

    9. D3 built: the run report (2026-10-24)¶

    route --report FILE writes one HTML (or Markdown) file per run, rendered from the route summary alone, so every number in it is a JSON field (decision D136, how-to Read a run report). It covers the candidate's list: what was routed; what is open and why (the stuck report of doc 17 §3 for the final result, with what each cause means, cut proofs, pins that cannot escape their package, and the items the refilled board leaves unconnected); the rules read and not read; the SI audit (critical nets, pairs, buses, return paths); the DRC of the board before and after (TraceMaker's DRC, zones refilled); the options as given and the seed. tracemaker report renders it again from a saved summary.

    Gate, as far as it goes (bench/records/m20-run-report): reports for all eight plane-tier boards, --soft-zones --si-rules, 3 M work. No routing error added on any board; the unconnected count after the refill equals the report's own DRC count on every board. A unit test checks every value of the HTML against its JSON pointer and that the renderer's own words hold no number. The extra work after the route took 0.1 to 2.1 s per board. Options off, the quick tier is byte-identical to main (30/30).

    Not met yet: the review by someone who did not write it. interf_u, which on 2026-10-24 spent more than 20 minutes on one core after its portfolio, finished in 128 s on 2026-10-27 after D137's GPU back-off (the stall was the GPU fallback cliff), and RoyalBlue54L-Feather was re-run under --time 3600 (72 s, not time-limited). Found on the way: under load the default 120 s time limit, not the work budget, ended some runs, and their results moved between runs; the summary now says so (time_limited) and the report warns.

    10. C3 built: generated pours and the layer-count advisor (2026-10-27)¶

    route --pour auto|NET@LAYER adds a ground (or named) pour to a board without zones and routes with soft zones; route --copper-layers N and tracemaker layers change the copper layer count; route --try-layers 2,4,6 routes each count, fewest first, and writes the first that completes (doc 05 §53, decision D138, records in bench/records/m20-c3).

    Gate: the advisor's answer equals a brute-force run (each count converted, routed and judged by KiCad) on 28 of 28 board-and-option pairs (14 two-layer boards, with and without a pour): met. Boards completed with a generated pour against without: 0 against 0 on the 33 non-clean tier B to D boards at their own layer counts (open connections after the refill 1,368 → 1,290), and 1 against 1 at 4 layers, 2 against 1 at 6 on the 14. The candidate's estimate holds: a pour closes few boards. On two layers the plane is cut up by the tracks that share its layer; the pour's thermal spokes, KiCad's default, were starved on 22 boards, so the pour connects pads solid.

    11. C2 measured: pin swap is not worth a pass (2026-10-27)¶

    15. B1 built: KiCad 10 tuning profiles (2026-10-28)¶

    The project reader reads KiCad 10's tuning profiles and compiles KiCad's implicit width and pair-gap rules, so the engine's DRC judges profiled tracks as KiCad does. Single-ended profiles are routed at their widest width and narrowed afterwards to each layer's width. Differential profiles keep their pairs on the layers that share one width and gap (doc 05 §58, decision D145, records in bench/records/m21-b1).

    KiCad 10.0.6 was measured where its behaviour and its source disagree, and the engine follows the behaviour:

    • an ignored profile rule still decides the width check;
    • hasExactNetclass never matches in a user's rule;
    • the coupled clearance at the profile's gap covers tracks, not pads.

    The DRC parity corpus has four new cases, and all 220 match KiCad.

    Gate (doc 18 §4 B1):

    • Boards with profiles route with each layer's profile width: met for single-ended profiles, and for pairs on the layers they keep.
    • kicad-cli with tuning_profile_track_geometries enabled adds 0 errors: met on the two boards given profiles. On RoyalBlue, routing with the single-ended profile alone is the same as without one. The pair profile costs 5 of 147 connections.
    • Parity corpus extended: met.
    • Options off byte-identical: the quick tier at the end of the night (morning report).

    No real board with profiles exists in the fixtures; the delays are left to B2.

    17. B2 built: time-domain matching (2026-10-28)¶

    The engine measures a net's length and delay as KiCad 10.0.6 does, quirks included, and checks length and skew rules in ps against the delay. The router tunes nets into ps windows and skew groups, weighting meanders by each layer's delay per mm. The DRC now also has KiCad's diff-pair coupling test, and the router avoids coupling-gap errors (doc 05 §59, decisions D147 and D148, records in bench/records/m21-b2).

    Checking B2 against KiCad showed that B1's fixtures were judged by a KiCad that read no profile, because their net settings were at schema 4. The router now avoids them (D148) and still routes 104 of 147 connections.

    18. Finishing the last connections: research and plan (2026-10-29, M22)¶

    The user asked what would take hard boards to 100 % routed. They had seen vias placed too close to footprint pins, connections that would route with two vias and a run on another layer, and paths that must first go the other way. A research round read the literature (escape routing, pin access, multi-layer search, negotiation), the source of Freerouting, tscircuit's capacity autorouter and KiCad's PNS router. It also measured 349 open connections on seven failing boards of the 0.12.1 release run, each routed alone on a copy of its final board (decision D155; report, notes and the viastudy harness in bench/records/m22-research).

    What the boards say:

    What the open connection needed to route alone Connections (of 349)
    Nothing: it routes on the final board, most with 0-2 vias (never attempted, ripped and not re-routed, or tried only while boxed in) 139
    More search: the whole-board window or more work (long detours, median 4-12 vias) about 15 more
    Other nets' copper within 2 mm of its pins removed (mostly tracks; vias alone rescue 46 of 288) 144 more
    Enclosed even with the whole board, any number of vias and every option about 52

    The search is not blind to layers: a via is possible at every lattice point and the cost-to-go field prices vias on every layer. What it lacks is a reflex. When it proves a pin boxed in, it moves on instead of clearing the space around the pin, and four of the seven boards never reached negotiation. Freerouting's recent gains come from the same place (LastMileBlockerRipup, RetryNeighbourhood: foreign copper ripped around open pins in a radius growing from 1.5 to 12 mm). None of the open routers prices other nets' vias in a footprint's escape region during the main route; on these boards that would help CoreOne and kitspace_d20 only.

    Plan (M22), in order. Each change sits behind an option, off until measured:

    # Change Evidence Effort
    1 Proof-triggered pin rescue: a pin proved boxed in by routed copper gets its neighbours within ~2 mm ripped, is routed first, and the victims are re-routed, as a transaction; on failure a seeded victim order and a radius growing toward ~12 mm copper near pins rescues 155 of 288; 229 of 526 open boxed in with no negotiation; --lns-open never got budget M
    2 Leave nothing unattempted: when the main loop ends, retry every ripped-not-re-routed and never-attempted connection on the current copper 139 of 349 route alone on the final board S
    3 Whole-board attempt for window misses (never for proved enclosures) whole-board window rescues 78; "no path in window" 133 of 526 S
    4 Informed pad-to-plane searches: a lower bound to the net's fill, the late proof, planning to nearby same-net copper; causes fills zones as route does pad-to-plane search floods to its 3 M cap (PoE switch C115.2, C123.2: 1.2 mm stubs) S-M
    5 D153 via retry only after budget misses, against an equal-budget control enclosure proofs do not depend on prices S
    6 Escape plan in more variants, then a via-only cost in dense pins' outward cones CoreOne vias 0.31 mm from open pins against 0.65 mm from routed ones S, then M
    7 A tight via state and off-lattice via sites the via margin has no tight fallback; neck-down vias carried 5 of CoreOne's 10 via rescues M
    8 Research: a via-site and layer planner for dense packages and buses (pin access points, via exclusion, crossing and layer planning) PAO, Bayless 2016, Haas 2026; start only if package fan-out is still the largest class L

    The user's "widen and randomise the last traces" is item 1's escalation: what widens is the radius and the order of what is ripped around one proved-stuck pin, seeded, not the search's cost weights (those were measured and lost, doc 16 §11).

    Gate: at least 15 % fewer open connections on pcbench-bd-fail at 10 M work, judged by KiCad; no clean board lost; quick and mid byte-identical with the options off.

    Items 1 and 2 (and part of 4) were built and measured on 2026-10-29: at the same budget, plain routing beat them (7,193 against 7,185 routed on ten failing boards; doc 05 §63). They stay as options, off.

    The rest of the plan was developed on one board, mini_mass_prog_bench_prog_rig, and then measured on the ten (2026-10-30, D156, doc 05 §64):

    • Item 7, as a via shove that moves other nets' vias near a boxed-in pin: mini_mass 986 → 987.
    • Items 3, 5 and 6 were also built.

    With --via-shove --escape-all the ten boards route 7,197, against 7,175 for the base and 7,193 for the base at 22 M. Open connections fall 3.5 %, short of the 15 % gate. The whole-board window (item 3) and the via penalty (item 6) lost, and stay off. Item 8 remains: on mini_mass the open pins are boxed in by their neighbours' tracks.

    Item 8 was then built as fan-out heads (D157, doc 05 §65): before routing, the pins of a dense four-sided package get the first millimetres of their routes as the designer lays them, straight or in staggered 45-degree bundles. mini_mass goes from 986 to 989 (the designer's own heads give 990). On the ten boards it nets zero (7,175, as the base) and stays off. On CoreOne even the designer's own heads do not help (785 against 787), so a better rule for the heads was not pursued.

    State of M22: items 1-3 and 5-8 are built and off by default, and item 4 is partly built (--plane-informed). The best arm, --via-shove --escape-all, cuts open connections by 3.5 % against the 15 % gate. Every repair so far lands within a few connections of the base given about 10 % more work, so more search per second is the stronger lever.

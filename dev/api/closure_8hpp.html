Detailed Description

Placement closure (design doc 04 §11, D190): move components until the router completes the board.

The input placement is routed; while connections stay unrouted, a multi-armed bandit chooses which kind of placement change to try next, from small ECO moves next to the failed connections to re-placing the whole board. One trial = one change of the incumbent placement, routed with the caller's deterministic work budget. A trial is kept only when it leaves fewer connections unrouted (transactions, CLAUDE.md rule 4). The bandit learns, on this board, which kinds pay off; a prior learned on other boards can be passed in, and this run's statistics are returned so the caller can store them.

Arms (the kinds of change), cheapest first: shift one part next to a failure, 1–2 pitches in x or y (doc 04 §4) rotate one part turned 90/180/270° about its body centre (doc 04 §4) swap two interchangeable parts exchange places (doc 04 §4) flip one part to the other side (only with –flip, D48) (doc 04 §4, §9) room every movable neighbour of a failed pad pushed away from it at once (pads the router reports as "boxed in" first): makes escape room no single-part move makes far one part 3–8 pitches away window refine annealing restricted to the parts near the failures, with the failed nets boosted and the routing capacity around them shrunk (doc 04 §3 G, one round) replace a new placement of the whole board (the caller's full-mode placement with the routability term; a different seed on each pull), from which the small arms carry on

Bandit: UCB1 (Auer, Cesa-Bianchi and Fischer, "Finite-time analysis of the multiarmed bandit problem", Machine Learning 47, 2002) on the success rate of each arm divided by its cost in routes, with a prior (pseudo-counts) so a run of 20–40 routes does not spend most of them exploring — the weakness of ALNS's segment-wise roulette weights (Ropke and Pisinger, Transportation Science 40(4), 2006) on short runs. After stall trials without improvement the next trial is radical (window or replace): the change escalates from slight to radical. The bandit's arithmetic is fixed-point, ties go to the lower arm index, and nothing depends on wall time except the job stop, so a run is reproducible.

3// Why a connection stays open (doc 20 §2.4): can a track get out of a pin at all, past the board's fixed copper?
4//
5// A track of width w whose centre keeps w/2 + c from every item of another net (c the rule clearance between the track and
6// that item) lives in the free space of the plane less those items, each grown by its own w/2 + c (the configuration space of
7// a disc; Lozano-Pérez, "Spatial planning: a configuration space approach", 1983). Two items whose copper gap is less than
8// w + c1 + c2 close the way between them. The pin is enclosed on a layer when the free space reachable from its copper
9// neither reaches other copper of the net nor leaves a window around the pin. Over every layer, and with no via site
10// (a via clear on all its layers) in the reachable space, no router can connect it as the parts are placed.
11//
12// The free space is sampled on a grid, and a cell counts as free when its centre is within half a cell diagonal of being
13// free: the sampled space is a superset of the true one, so "enclosed" is a proof; "not enclosed" is not a promise.
14// Only the board's own copper counts (pads, existing tracks and vias, zone fills unless soft, holes, the edge). Copper on the pin
15// itself is no wall: it is the pin's own pour, or a short already there. Net-less copper graphics on it are walls to the router
16// (without --soft-copper-graphics), and a pin walled in only by them or by zone fills is reported as such, not as enclosed: the routed
17// copper of other nets is what a router may move, so a pin that is not enclosed here is congested, not blocked.

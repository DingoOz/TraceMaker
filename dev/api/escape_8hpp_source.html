3// Escape (fanout) planning, version 1 (roadmap M9; design doc 05 §3).
4//
5// Dense packages (QFP/TSOP/SOIC rows, BGA/LGA arrays) fail in the router's strict first pass when other nets'
6// routes take the channels a pin needs to leave its package: the pin is then "boxed in" until negotiation,
7// which large boards often do not reach within the budget. The plan reserves, for every pin that has to be
8// routed, a short escape corridor: straight out of the package for pins on its perimeter, and a dog-bone stub to
9// the diagonal interstitial via site for the inner balls of an SMD array (the classic BGA fanout; Yan & Wong,
10// "Recent research development in PCB layout", ICCAD 2010, §3; Kong, Yan & Wong, "Optimal simultaneous pin
11// assignment and escape routing for dense PCBs", ASP-DAC 2010, for the escape model). The router treats
12// another net's corridor as blocked in strict passes and as costly in negotiated passes, and releases a corridor
13// once its pin is connected. Reservations only remove options: every commit is still checked exactly, so the
14// plan cannot introduce violations.

57// Plans corridors for the pads with needs[pad] != 0. keep(net) is the distance another net's centreline must
58// keep from this net's centreline (track width + clearance); the band is that, capped at a share of the pin
59// pitch so the corridors of neighbouring pins never overlap. Deterministic: footprints and pads in board order.
60// channel(net), when given, is the gap a track of that net needs between two pads (narrowest legal width plus
61// twice the clearance): second-ring balls whose outer neighbours leave that much room escape on their own layer
62// between them (the classic two-ring fanout) instead of taking a dog-bone via.

69// Escape feasibility (M9 analysis): can each pin of a dense package leave it at all under the board's rules?
70// A breadth-first search from the pad over a lattice of the package area, against fixed copper only (nothing
71// routed; fills as the obstacles are set: hard, or ignored with set_soft_zones), at the narrowest legal width
72// (the router's neck-down width), changing layers wherever that net's via passes every fixed check. The router's
73// custom-rule limits apply: per-net track layers and via permission, and position-dependent disallow rules.
74// A pin escapes when it reaches `margin` outside the box of the package's pad centres. Pins whose net is already
75// complete in the existing copper are `satisfied`, not searched; complete as the router's planner sees it, so with
76// soft zones a plane of the net that no pad touches yet leaves the net outstanding. The search is complete over
77// its finite lattice and window, so "dead" means "no escape on this lattice": an off-lattice path can exist in
78// rare cases (the router keeps trying those pins), and a pin that escapes alone may still lose its channel to its
79// neighbours.
80struct DeadPin {
81 int pad = -1;
82 std::string reason;
83};

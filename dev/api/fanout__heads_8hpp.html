Detailed Description

Fan-out heads (M22 item 8, D157; design doc 05 §65): the first millimetres of each perimeter pin's route out of a dense four-sided package, laid as the designer lays them before anything else is routed.

On mini_mass_prog_bench_prog_rig the designer's own copper within 5 mm of the QFN-44 (only the parts that touch one pad), put into the unrouted board, let the router reach 990 of 995 connections where it otherwise stops at 986: the other nets route round the escapes instead of putting a ring of vias 1-2 mm out that boxes the pins in. Straight stubs from every pin did worse (983); what the designer does is to leave each side either straight or in staggered 45-degree bundles toward the pins' destinations, and to keep a side straight where the bundles would run into the parts beside the package.

The heads are planar by construction (the ordered escape of Kong, Yan and Wong, "Optimal simultaneous pin assignment and escape routing for dense PCBs", ASP-DAC 2010, restricted to one bend per pin): along a side the pins split into a block that turns toward the low end, a straight block and a block that turns toward the high end, and each turning pin bends one step further out than its neighbour toward the corner, so parallel diagonals keep track width plus clearance apart. Every leg is cut at the first point the exact check refuses; a head that ran into something stops short of it, so its own route keeps room to leave.

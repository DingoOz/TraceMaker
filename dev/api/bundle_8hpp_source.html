3// Bundle (river) escapes (design doc 05 §50). The connections that leave one side of a dense package (QFN, QFP, SO) for one side of another part are
4// routed as one block, the way a designer draws them: parallel straight tracks at the pin pitch, in the order of the pins, for as long as the board has
5// room, and each track peels off toward its own pad at the last moment. Not a search per connection: ONE search for the spine of the block (a track as
6// wide as the whole block, on an octilinear grid, turns spaced so that the inner tracks of a turn keep their length), then every lane is the spine offset
7// by the lane's distance from the middle (rigid offsets: the order of the pins is the order of the lanes, so lanes cannot cross), and a last leg from the
8// lane to its pad. A lane is placed only when every piece of it passes the router's own exact check; a connection that does not get a lane is left to
9// the ordinary router. Related work: river routing (Hsu, "A new approach to channel routing"; Tsukiyama and Kuh, "Single-layer routing"); the
10// generalisation of the router's coupled-pair legs (doc 05 §15) from two tracks to n.

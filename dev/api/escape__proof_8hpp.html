Detailed Description

Why a connection stays open (doc 20 §2.4): can a track get out of a pin at all, past the board's fixed copper?

A track of width w whose centre keeps w/2 + c from every item of another net (c the rule clearance between the track and that item) lives in the free space of the plane less those items, each grown by its own w/2 + c (the configuration space of a disc; Lozano-Pérez, "Spatial planning: a configuration space approach", 1983). Two items whose copper gap is less than w + c1 + c2 close the way between them. The pin is enclosed on a layer when the free space reachable from its copper neither reaches other copper of the net nor leaves a window around the pin. Over every layer, and with no via site (a via clear on all its layers) in the reachable space, no router can connect it as the parts are placed.

The free space is sampled on a grid, and a cell counts as free when its centre is within half a cell diagonal of being free: the sampled space is a superset of the true one, so "enclosed" is a proof; "not enclosed" is not a promise. Only the board's own copper counts (pads, existing tracks and vias, zone fills unless soft, holes, the edge). Copper on the pin itself is no wall: it is the pin's own pour, or a short already there. Net-less copper graphics on it are walls to the router (without –soft-copper-graphics), and a pin walled in only by them or by zone fills is reported as such, not as enclosed: the routed copper of other nets is what a router may move, so a pin that is not enclosed here is congested, not blocked.

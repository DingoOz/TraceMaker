3// Placement closure (design doc 04 §11, D190): move components until the router completes the board.
4//
5// The input placement is routed; while connections stay unrouted, a multi-armed bandit chooses which kind of
6// placement change to try next, from small ECO moves next to the failed connections to re-placing the whole board.
7// One trial = one change of the incumbent placement, routed with the caller's deterministic work budget. A trial is
8// kept only when it leaves fewer connections unrouted (transactions, CLAUDE.md rule 4). The bandit learns, on this
9// board, which kinds pay off; a prior learned on other boards can be passed in, and this run's statistics are
10// returned so the caller can store them.
11//
12// Arms (the kinds of change), cheapest first:
13// shift one part next to a failure, 1–2 pitches in x or y (doc 04 §4)
14// rotate one part turned 90/180/270° about its body centre (doc 04 §4)
15// swap two interchangeable parts exchange places (doc 04 §4)
16// flip one part to the other side (only with --flip, D48) (doc 04 §4, §9)
17// room every movable neighbour of a failed pad pushed away from it at once (pads the router reports as
18// "boxed in" first): makes escape room no single-part move makes
19// far one part 3–8 pitches away
20// window refine annealing restricted to the parts near the failures, with the failed nets boosted and the
21// routing capacity around them shrunk (doc 04 §3 G, one round)
22// replace a new placement of the whole board (the caller's full-mode placement with the routability term; a
23// different seed on each pull), from which the small arms carry on
24//
25// Bandit: UCB1 (Auer, Cesa-Bianchi and Fischer, "Finite-time analysis of the multiarmed bandit problem", Machine
26// Learning 47, 2002) on the success rate of each arm divided by its cost in routes, with a prior (pseudo-counts) so
27// a run of 20–40 routes does not spend most of them exploring — the weakness of ALNS's segment-wise roulette
28// weights (Ropke and Pisinger, Transportation Science 40(4), 2006) on short runs. After `stall` trials without
29// improvement the next trial is radical (window or replace): the change escalates from slight to radical. The
30// bandit's arithmetic is fixed-point, ties go to the lower arm index, and nothing depends on wall time except the
31// job stop, so a run is reproducible.

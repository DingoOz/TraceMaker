Detailed Description

Cut test of a routed board's topology (doc 05 §67, D159).

Cole and Siegel (river routing, 1984) and Leiserson and Maley ("Algorithms for routing and testing routability of planar VLSI layouts", STOC 1985) show that the wires of a one-layer routing can be moved, keeping their topology, to meet given widths and clearances exactly when every cut has room: for every two features (pads, vias) that see each other, the wires that must pass between them, with their widths and the clearances between neighbours, fit in the gap. Gao, Jerrum, Kaufmann, Mehlhorn and Rülling ("On continuous homotopic one layer routing", 1988) give the condition for continuous geometry. This module checks those cuts on every copper layer of a routed board:

  • a cut's wires are the crossings of the routed centrelines with the shortest segment between the two features, less pairs of crossings of one wire that enclose no feature (the wire can be pulled out of the cut);
  • its need is the sum of the wire widths and of the rule clearances between neighbours along the cut (copper of one net may overlap, so wires of an end's own net next to it and runs of one net merge);
  • an over-full cut proves that no geometry with this topology and these feature positions meets the rules.

Vias are features of the topology here (the sketch keeps them where they are). The via room test asks the weaker question with the via free to move inside its cell (it may not cross a cut between two pads): if no position keeps it clear of the fixed pads with the wires the topology puts between them, the via has no room, whatever the geometry. Necessary conditions only: wire-to-wire spacing away from features, zones and the board edge are not cuts here.

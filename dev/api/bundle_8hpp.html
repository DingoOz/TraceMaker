Detailed Description

Bundle (river) escapes (design doc 05 §50).

The connections that leave one side of a dense package (QFN, QFP, SO) for one side of another part are routed as one block, the way a designer draws them: parallel straight tracks at the pin pitch, in the order of the pins, for as long as the board has room, and each track peels off toward its own pad at the last moment. Not a search per connection: ONE search for the spine of the block (a track as wide as the whole block, on an octilinear grid, turns spaced so that the inner tracks of a turn keep their length), then every lane is the spine offset by the lane's distance from the middle (rigid offsets: the order of the pins is the order of the lanes, so lanes cannot cross), and a last leg from the lane to its pad. A lane is placed only when every piece of it passes the router's own exact check; a connection that does not get a lane is left to the ordinary router. Related work: river routing (Hsu, "A new approach to channel routing"; Tsukiyama and Kuh, "Single-layer routing"); the generalisation of the router's coupled-pair legs (doc 05 §15) from two tracks to n.

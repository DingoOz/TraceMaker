3// Cut test of a routed board's topology (doc 05 §67, D159).
4//
5// Cole and Siegel (river routing, 1984) and Leiserson and Maley ("Algorithms for routing and testing routability of
6// planar VLSI layouts", STOC 1985) show that the wires of a one-layer routing can be moved, keeping their topology, to
7// meet given widths and clearances exactly when every cut has room: for every two features (pads, vias) that see each
8// other, the wires that must pass between them, with their widths and the clearances between neighbours, fit in the gap.
9// Gao, Jerrum, Kaufmann, Mehlhorn and Rülling ("On continuous homotopic one layer routing", 1988) give the condition
10// for continuous geometry. This module checks those cuts on every copper layer of a routed board:
11//
12// - a cut's wires are the crossings of the routed centrelines with the shortest segment between the two features,
13// less pairs of crossings of one wire that enclose no feature (the wire can be pulled out of the cut);
14// - its need is the sum of the wire widths and of the rule clearances between neighbours along the cut (copper of one
15// net may overlap, so wires of an end's own net next to it and runs of one net merge);
16// - an over-full cut proves that no geometry with this topology and these feature positions meets the rules.
17//
18// Vias are features of the topology here (the sketch keeps them where they are). The via room test asks the weaker
19// question with the via free to move inside its cell (it may not cross a cut between two pads): if no position keeps it
20// clear of the fixed pads with the wires the topology puts between them, the via has no room, whatever the geometry.
21// Necessary conditions only: wire-to-wire spacing away from features, zones and the board edge are not cuts here.

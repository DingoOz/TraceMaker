    16 — Plan: matching tscircuit on BGA breakout boards¶

    Status: Partly built

    Part of this page describes code that exists; the rest is a plan. The page says which is which.

    Part of the TraceMaker plan. Start at ../PLAN.md. Written 2026-10-11.

    Status: partly built (2026-10-11, §10 has the outcomes). §1–§9 are the plan as written before any experiment; §10 records what the experiments found and what was built. Where §1–§9 say "TraceMaker will", read it as a proposal. The evidence is in ../research/bga-routing-2026-10.md; evidence tags [V] verified, [A] abstract only, [C] calculated are used the same way here. Proposed milestone: M14. This note changes no code, so it adds no row to 12-decisions.md; the decisions it proposes (§7) get their rows when they are made.

    1. Goal and scoreboard¶

    Goal. Route at least as many of tscircuit's BGA boards as tscircuit's router does, judged by KiCad's DRC (and, as a second score, tscircuit's own relaxed checker), without adding a DRC error to any board.

    Track Set tscircuit (dashboard run 38105514608-1, commit 65522c8, 2026-10-11, 1x effort) TraceMaker today (doc 10 §7, 50 M work, one variant, one thread) Gate
    A srj19 (200 BGA breakouts, 2 layers, MIT) 92.0 % completed, 90.0 % relaxed pass (184 / 180 boards) 79.5 % clean (159 boards) ≥ 184 clean and ≥ 180 relaxed
    A srj20 (200 BGAs with missing balls, 2 layers, no licence: local use only) 78.5 % / 78.5 % (157 boards) 50.0 % clean (100 boards) ≥ 157 clean and ≥ 157 relaxed
    B fanout31 (72 SoC fan-out cases, 8-layer fixtures) 48 / 72 solved (committed report 2026-09-09, 120 s per case) not run; no importer for the boundary-port semantics ≥ 48 / 72 (same cases, all balls validated)

    Rules for the comparison:

    • Pin the opponent. tscircuit's rates moved from 91.5 / 77 to 92.0 / 78.5 within a day. Every quoted figure names a dashboard run id; bench/ stores the per-board JSON of the pinned run so results can be joined board by board.
    • Matched effort, stated. tscircuit's setting is 360 s per board on a 32-vCPU runner (median 19–23 s). Report two rows: TraceMaker at the current standard budgets, and at ≤ 360 s wall on ≤ 32 threads (the portfolio is allowed; it is how this router uses cores).
    • Tune / hold out. Tune on samples 1–100 of each set, report on all 200, and keep 101–200 untouched until a change is final (CLAUDE.md's held-out rule; the sets are public and small enough to overfit).
    • Never lose a board. Three srj19 boards (24, 54, 126) are clean in TraceMaker and fail tscircuit's check; keep them. A change that drops a currently clean board is a regression (rule 9).

    2. What exists (and where)¶

    The SRJ importer/exporter, the two benchmark sets and the scorer (D90) live on branch t6-srj (commit c00c71a), and bus routing (D91) and after-route passes (D92) on srj-bus-postroute; none of it is on main. M14.0 below merges it. The router, escape corridors (--escape-plan), the flow planner (--escape-flow, doc 05 §14), the escape feasibility analysis (tracemaker escape), differential pairs (D50) and zone-aware routing (§26) are on main.

    3. What the data says¶

    All figures: the 400 boards of srj19/srj20, TraceMaker at 50 M work from build/t6-srj/bench/results/*-w50m, tscircuit from the pinned dashboard run. Computed in this session [C].

    3.1 The gap is a cliff in board size, not a general weakness.

    Connections per board srj19: boards TM tscircuit srj20: boards TM tscircuit
    < 20 26 100 % 100 % 25 100 % 100 %
    20–39 94 100 % 99 % 74 92 % 100 %
    40–59 60 63 % 82 % 57 12 % 88 %
    60–79 17 6 % 71 % 30 0 % 23 %
    ≥ 80 3 0 % 0 % 14 0 % 7 %

    By straight-line crossings between a board's connections (a proxy for how many escapes must swap sides): below 100 crossings TraceMaker passes all 103 srj19 boards and all 64 srj20 boards (tscircuit: 102 and 64); at 100 or more crossings TraceMaker passes 58 % (56 of 97 boards, srj19) and 26 % (36 of 136, srj20) against tscircuit's 80 % and 68 %. The boards TraceMaker loses and tscircuit wins have a median of 331 (srj19) and 289 (srj20) crossings.

    3.2 The misses are not near-misses. Of the 81 boards that tscircuit solves and TraceMaker does not, 32 leave more than ten connections open, 24 leave six to ten, and only 11 leave one or two. A last-few-connections tool (exact window solve, group re-route: doc 05 §17 found no effect) cannot close most of the gap.

    3.3 Per-ball escape is not the limit. tracemaker escape reports every ball escapable on every board checked (srj19 samples 11, 19, 31, 117 fail in both routers); escape --flow plans samples 19 and 117 entirely on the pad layer. A ball can leave alone and the set of balls still cannot be completed: the connections have fixed destinations (I/O pads around the package, passives on the other side), so the escapes must also arrive in a compatible order, and the unordered flow model ignores that (research L2).

    3.4 More search does not close it, and the escape plans do not help. srj19 stops improving between 50 M and 200 M (88 % on its first 50 boards); the eight-variant portfolio at 10 M equals one variant at 50 M; --escape-plan and --escape-flow lower both sets' clean rate (66 → 60 % and 34 → 32 / 28 %). srj20 is still rising with budget (46 → 56 % at 200 M), so part of that set is budget-limited.

    3.5 tscircuit's router is topology first. Its stages: escape-via location → topology planning → a capacity-mesh global path per connection (1.8–2.2 s of ~70–105 s) → per-cell high-density routing (37–41 % of time) → force improve (27–36 %) → DRC repair (12–21 %). Most of its failures (8 of 20, 35 of 43) are timeouts inside force improve. It uses more vias: 72 per board against TraceMaker's 44 on the 156 srj19 boards both solve (57 against 40 on the 100 srj20 boards both solve; 66 % and 43 % more) [C].

    3.6 Geometry. At 0.8 mm pitch a channel holds one track, and a through via at an interstice closes the two channels it touches on both layers (research §A). On two layers every escape is a channel or a via site; they compete.

    4. Hypotheses and the experiments that test them¶

    None of the explanations below is established. The first milestone runs the cheap experiments, and the build list in §5 is ordered by what they find. A hypothesis is dropped when its experiment fails its stated test.

    # Hypothesis Experiment Supported if / dropped if

    E1 is the deciding experiment. It separates "TraceMaker needs a topology planner" from "TraceMaker's detailed search cannot realise a good topology", which need different work. It uses tscircuit's code only as a measurement instrument on public data; nothing of it enters the engine.

    5. Features to add, in order¶

    Order follows what E1–E5 are expected to show; reorder once they have run. Each item lands with its reference path and equivalence test (rule 3), integer fixed-point costs and fixed tie-breaks (rule 2), and is an option or portfolio arm until the quick tier and the srj tune half show no regression (rule 9).

    F0. Tooling (M14.0; no router change)¶

    1. Merge t6-srj and srj-bus-postroute into main. Quick tier must stay byte-identical (D90 reports it so).
    2. bench/srj_vs_tscircuit.py: join a TraceMaker run with a stored dashboard run JSON (data/runs/<id>.json, one file kept under bench/data/tscircuit/dashboard/, not committed) and print the 2×2 table, the gap boards, and per-bucket rates as in §3.
    3. Failure classifier in bench/run.py output: per open connection, "no channel / no via site / ripped too often / budget" from the router's existing reason strings, so progress can be attributed.
    4. A crossings column in the board JSON (the proxy of §3.1), so a new board's difficulty is known before routing.

    F1. Package-scale breakout planner (the main build; depends on E1 supporting H1)¶

    A planner for ordered escape on the pad-gap graph, run before the search and used as guidance.

    • Graph. Nodes: the gaps and interstices between pads (as escape_flow already builds them), with capacities from exact geometry including the diagonal (research L1); via sites as layer-change edges that remove the channels they touch (3.6); a few coarse nodes outside the package towards each destination pad.
    • Problem. Each connection is a commodity with a fixed source ball and destination pad (research L2). Choose a path of (layer, gap) steps. Crossings between paths on one layer are forbidden; a crossing is resolved by giving one path a via site and the other layer.
    • Method. Negotiated congestion (PathFinder: McMurchie and Ebeling, FPGA 1995, already the basis of doc 05) over the gap graph with via-site and channel capacities as the shared resources; a one-layer planar subset first (Ozdal and Wong's maximal planar subset, research §B) as the initial layer assignment, the rest to the other layer.
    • Output as guidance, not reservation. A cost bonus along the planned path, and a fixed-cost via site per connection that needs one; never corridors that block other nets. Reservations are the thing that made every earlier escape plan lose (M1, M2, M3).
    • Reference and equality. With guidance disabled the router is byte-identical to today's. A test builds boards where the plan is trivial (one ring) and checks the plan equals the old corridors' escape.
    • Kill criterion. If E1 shows the oracle topology is not enough (H1 dropped), do not build F1; do F2/F3 first.

    F2. Joint via-site assignment (depends on H2)¶

    escapeViaLocationSolver is tscircuit's first stage. Match balls that need a via to interstice sites in one min-cost matching: cost by distance and by how many channels the site closes, hard constraint that a via and a track through the same interstice cannot coexist (3.6), and a joint choice rather than greedy (tscircuit's own fix, research T7). Extends the dog-bone corridors of doc 05 §12 from "pointing away from the centre" to a matched set. If E2 supports H2 with only a via-cost change, F2 is that cost change and stops there.

    F3. Channel-centred pad stubs and a finer last rung (depends on H3)¶

    Escape stubs that centre the track in the channel, and a half-pitch retry of a whole fan-out (not a window) before the net is declared boxed in. Small, local to pad_cells and the escalation rungs.

    F4. Compute plumbing (depends on H4)¶

    Run the portfolio with the cores the machine has and a wall-time budget of 360 s; report work and wall separately (D86). No algorithm change; it fixes how srj20 is reported against a 32-vCPU opponent.

    F5. A joint feasibility certificate (E5; independent)¶

    Extend --cut-report to the package: per layer, capacity of the pad-gap graph across a cut around the package against the number of connections that must cross it, with the over-full cut printed. Also gives infeasibility proofs without a SAT solver. A SAT/CP window solver (research L4, L7) is not proposed: only 11 of the 81 gap boards are within two connections of done (3.2), and doc 05 §20 already found an exact window model would mostly answer "feasible, the heuristic just did not find it". Revisit if F1 leaves many near-misses.

    F6. Gridless polish (low priority; depends on H3 and on F1 leaving clearance-limited failures)¶

    tscircuit spends 27–36 % of its time in a force-based improver, and it is where most of its timeouts are. A TraceMaker equivalent would be a transaction (rule 4) that nudges committed tracks and vias apart inside a window to open a channel, accepted only if the exact check passes and an open connection then routes. Build only if E3 shows clearance-limited failures; otherwise it adds work without moving the gate.

    Track B: fan-out of SoC packages (fanout31)¶

    A different problem (research §A): a prefix, with boundary exits, plane drops and bus rules, on 4–10 layers. Needed, in order:

    1. B0 baseline. Convert the 72 cases from dataset-fanout31-am62l with the srj-import path (the SRJ in fanout-solver/tests/fixtures/*.json has simpleRouteJson, solverOptions, buses, differentialPairs). Add set fanout31 and report solved / 72 with "every ball validated" as the pass rule, 120 s per case.
    2. B1 boundary ports. A connection whose second point is on the shared breakout edge: the end may slide along the edge, exit order is fixed by the bus, and the route ends exactly on the edge. Model as a row of virtual pads on the edge (off-board edge keep-in), not as a new rule.
    3. B2 plane drops. termination: plane is a via to an inner plane layer; doc 05 §26 (plane-aware routing) covers the router side. The 102–117 plane balls per case compete with signals for interstice sites, so F2 (joint via-site assignment) matters most here.
    4. B3 buses and skew. D91 (buses, skew) and D50 (differential pairs) exist on branches; check that atomic bus layer, all-or-none vias, 45° only and the length-skew limits are what they enforce.
    5. B4 capacity. 8–10 layers at 0.08128 mm tracks: lattice memory and the cost-to-go fields. Measure first.
    6. B5 geometric pre-check. The BGA400 four-layer wall (research T1) means some cases are unroutable under the stated rules; the F5 certificate is the check.

    tscircuit solves AM62L 12 / 12, K230 12 / 12, RK3308 9 / 12, i.MX6ULL 11 / 12, T113-S3 4 / 12 and AM3352 0 / 12. All of AM62L, K230, RK3308 and i.MX6ULL (48 cases) would match the count without T113-S3 or AM3352. Track B is independent of Track A and should start only after M14.1's findings, because F2 serves both.

    7. Decisions proposed (to be recorded when made)¶

    1. Planner output is guidance only, never reservation (research M1–M3).
    2. tscircuit's code is a measurement tool, not a dependency, and its datasets are fetched, never copied (D90).
    3. Exact solvers are used for certificates and small windows, not as the main method (research L7, doc 05 §20).
    4. Learned components are not a dependency (research L10; rule: learned arms only through the bandit).

    9. Open questions¶

    1. Whether the unsolved 17 / 43 boards are infeasible (E5).
    2. What tscircuit's force-improve stage does; its write-up was not found, so F6 is a guess from the timing.
    3. Whether a 36–54 ball srj board's difficulty comes from the destination order or from the passives on the other side (srj19 failures have a median of 42 bottom-layer destinations against 8–9 on successes; srj20 has none on the bottom). E1 and the bucket tables should be split by that too.
    4. Whether fanout31's fixtures are the cases tscircuit's committed 48 / 72 refers to: the committed report is a month older than the latest fixes, so the gate may need re-pinning once B0 runs.

    10. Outcomes (2026-10-11)¶

    Measured on this machine (bench/run.py, KiCad's DRC as the judge, one variant, no GPU, work budgets; the gap sets are bench/sets/srj19-gap.txt and srj20-gap.txt: the 24 and 57 boards tscircuit's router solves and TraceMaker at 50 M did not).

    Hypotheses.

    # Result
    H1 topology (E1) Not supported. tscircuit's own routes, run locally (bun, AutoroutingPipelineSolver9) on 12 gap boards and given as soft or hard path guides (--guide, radii 1.5–8 pitches): 440–499 of 570 connections routed, baseline 499. Guiding a connection along tscircuit's geometry does not complete the board; the lattice cannot reproduce its off-grid copper and the neighbours' lanes then differ. As a sanity check, TraceMaker's own complete solution as a guide reproduced only 36–40 of 41
    H2 vias (E2) Weak. Via cost 3 → 0.3 mm: gap boards 76.7 → 80.9 % of connections, 0 → 2 clean of 24. tscircuit uses 43–66 % more vias, but cheaper vias alone do not close the gap
    H3 lattice (E3) Dropped. 20 µm lattice 61.6 % (below 76.7 %), 50 µm 78.9 %, 60 µm 77.9 % at equal work
    H4 budget (E4) Supported for srj19: the 24 gap boards reach 95.7 % of connections and 6 clean at 200 M (median 192 s), and most remaining failures are one to three connections. At 10 M the strict first pass and its reachability floods use the whole budget (flood cells count 1:1 toward --work), so negotiation never runs
    H5 feasibility (E5) Not built. The failures TraceMaker leaves are dominated by negotiation churn ("ripped up by X and not routed again": 585 of about 1,170 srj20 failures), not by proven infeasibility

A planner that decides layer and via sites per connection before routing (F1, F2 of §5) is still the untried candidate; the cheap proxies above (via cost, package penalty, layer assignment, guides) did not stand in for it.

    Track B (fanout31). Built: bench/prepare_fanout31.py, bench/fanout31_run.py, and importer features (doc 08 §9): plane terminations become zones, breakout points take the layer their bus gives in connectionExitTargets, pads of a net that no connection point lies on become keep-outs (--unpointed-keepout; the problem asks only for the fan-out at the SoC), --outline-margin-mm, and the extension tracemakerTrackLayers. The full 72-case result is in §10.1 once the run has finished.

    12. Third round (M15, 2026-10-11 evening): negotiation as transactions¶

    The goal was srj20 (133/200 against tscircuit's 157). Fifteen ideas were tried at equal work; one of them, and an accounting correction that came with it, moved the numbers. Raw numbers: dev/m15-notes.md; assumptions taken without asking: dev/assumptions-m15.md.

    What was found about plain negotiation. On a 53-connection board (srj20 sample 069, 30 M work) the routed count of the negotiation phase wobbles between 30 and 41 for the whole budget and the run ends at 30; only the best snapshot (41) is returned. A connection that cannot be routed rips the 1–10 connections in its way (median 4), those cannot all be routed again, and the next one rips them back: the oscillation PathFinder-type negotiation is known for (research note C5). The open connections that remain on boards with 1–2 open are mostly outer-ring balls with a partner pad 10–17 mm away across the board, not deep balls: the long connections are the ones left without a corridor.

    Transactions (--txn, D99). A negotiated route that rips victims is kept only if the victims can be routed again strictly (without ripping anything) and the net change in routed connections is at least zero (--txn 1; --txn 2 needs a gain). Otherwise the whole board goes back to the state before the route (a snapshot of the copper of every connection; history stays, so the next try takes another path). This is MAPF-LNS2's acceptance rule applied to rip-up and re-route. A route that loses k connections net is still kept with probability exp(−k/T) (--txn-temp T, seeded counter-based draws: deterministic); T = 1.5 on breakout boards.

On the 76 boards that one variant at 50 M leaves open: 100 M plain 5 clean, with transactions 9.

    Budget accounting and reachability floods. --work counts a reachability-flood cell like an A* expansion, but a flood cell costs a small fraction of the time (field construction and the A* itself dominate). With --reach-check 2 (flood before every strict search) and a flood cell counted at a quarter of an expansion (--flood-work-pct 25), 30 M work buys about twice the search: 30 of 57 clean (52.6 %) in 137 s CPU per board, against 23 in 106 s for transactions alone and 23 in 71 s for plain negotiation.

    Engine speed. The cost-to-go field was 47 % of the time of a transaction run. Its inputs (pass/via masks from the fixed-copper cache, target cells, costs) are identical for repeated searches of one connection once the fixed-copper cache is complete, so --field-prefill 1 (fill the cache for the whole board at the first field) with --field-cache-mb (keep fields, reuse on an identical input, confirmed by a byte comparison) gives the same copper (sample 123: byte-identical) in 62 % of the time (hits 236 of 300 fields). It is not byte-identical on ordinary boards (6 of the 30 quick boards differ: a stronger heuristic breaks ties differently), so it is part of the breakout profile only. gprof of a transaction run: search() 26 % self, field_cpu 20 %, routed_copper_part 16 %, point_state 7.5 %.

    Breakout profile (D100). On a breakout board (D98) the router now also uses: --txn 1 --txn-temp 1.5, --reach-check 2, --flood-work-pct 25, --field-prefill 1, --field-cache-mb 512. Explicit options win; --breakout-profile 0 turns the last four off. Elsewhere nothing changed: quick (30) and mid (11) are byte-identical (ctest 262 → see final checks).

    Portfolio on a breakout board (D101). With --variants > 1 a board that looks like a breakout gets its own list instead of the designed eight (exact bends, longest first and the 2× pitch variants are wrong for it): variant 0 is the plain profile, the others draw order (shortest first, deep pins first, jittered), via cost, crossing cost, direction strength, package penalty and temperature with a counter-based generator. --rounds R (automatic: 4) splits the budget; after each round every variant starts from the best state of all variants (their copper per connection is a warm start, matched by the two pads of each connection) and negotiates from there with its own parameters. Selection is by the usual total order, so the result does not depend on thread timing. On 39 of the 76 boards one variant leaves open (16 variants × 30 M, 16 threads, 107–117 s wall per board, no GPU): the designed list with the profile 11 clean and 342 connections open in total; the breakout list 12 clean, 319 open; with four rounds 12 clean, 284 open. Rounds improve the boards that stay open more than they complete new ones.

    Measured and dropped (hidden options stay, D94 practice): lattice pitch 36.7/44/45/55 µm (neutral, ±3 boards of noise), victims re-routed in several orders (--txn-orders: 4 and 1 clean of 76 against 7), cap on victim re-route searches (--txn-cap), tidy sweeps (--tidy: re-route each connection alone, keep if shorter: 10–20 shortened per sweep, no extra boards), stall restarts (--stall-passes), net orders 1/2/3 under the new profile, per-net routed-state cache (identical, no speed-up).

    13. Fourth round (2026-10-13): the plateau was the lattice, not the capacity (D103, D104)¶

    Question. After M15 the single configuration stood at srj19 191/200 and srj20 160/200, and up to 199 and about 168 over many long runs; 28 srj20 boards of 70+ connections sat 8-40 connections short and six of them (039, 048, 071, 072, 103, 104) at 1-6 open after three-hour runs that no longer improved. Was that a capacity limit (not enough room between the balls for the tracks) or a search limit?

    Measurements (srj20 boards 039, 048, 071, 072, 103, 104; 16-variant portfolio, until complete, 30 min):

    Rules Routed of 453 Complete
    true (0.10 mm clearance), 3 h 436 0 (1-5 open each)
    clearance 0.08 mm 434 0
    clearance 0.06 mm (two tracks fit in a pad gap and between two vias) 453 6, in 3-27 min
    via 0.25 mm / drill 0.12, clearance 0.1 (via gap 0.55 holds two tracks) 435 0
    ball pads 0.26 mm instead of 0.36, clearance 0.1 (pad gap 0.54 holds two tracks) 421 0

    Doubling one gap alone did nothing and the step to 0.06 completed everything, which looked like a capacity wall. It is not: (i) warm-starting a run from the 0.06 mm solution completes sample048 at 0.07, 0.08 and 0.085 mm and sample071 at 0.065, rules at which scratch runs stay one connection short; (ii) the 0.085 mm solution of sample048 has no pad gap with two tracks, four via gaps with two (0.9 mm apart, which hold two at 0.10 anyway), and all 131 pairs that are too close at 0.10 mm are 0.087-0.098 mm squeezes; (iii) moving the vertices of the 0.06 mm solutions off the lattice until the 0.10 mm rules hold (route/legalize.cpp, doc 05 §41.3) leaves 071, 104 and (with more sweeps) 039 without a single pair too close, and 103, 048, 072 with 3-9.

    Result. Relax, route, legalise (--relax, D104): stage 1 routes under 60 % of the clearance, the solution is legalised to 80 %, the missing connections routed, legalised to 100 %, and a stall at the real rules sends the run back two stages. On the six boards, with the 0.06 mm solutions as the start: 071 (0.2 s), 039, 103 and 048 complete under the real rules in 3-13 min, KiCad DRC with no router-introduced error and nothing unconnected, boards on which three hours of scratch search had stopped with 1-5 connections open.

    Reading. The lattice router puts tracks on lattice points (0.035 mm) and cannot move committed routes, so a solution needs more room than the continuous geometry does (spacing 7 steps = 0.245 mm for a 0.22 mm requirement, vias and pad escapes on points); the room is there, it is just not reachable by a router that only commits lattice paths. The remaining failures are local pinches of a topology (a via between a pad and the board edge, a track between a via and a pad) that need a different topology of the nets around them, which the router then has to find.

    • Did not help on the last missing connection of sample104/072/031: islands (variants continue from their own stage solutions), finer ramp steps (0.01 mm), a half-pitch lattice (0.0175 mm) for the repair rounds, more projection sweeps (3,000 instead of 300).

# Free boxed-in pins before adding layer smarts

TraceMaker's hard boards will get closer to 100 % from an end game that moves other nets' copper away from stuck pins, retries what the run gave up on, and widens the search window only for connections that hit its edge. A more layer-aware search will not do it, because the router already has one. The A\* already considers a through via at every lattice point on every layer. Its cost-to-go field already prices layer changes. Its "boxed in" verdict is an exact proof over all layers and any number of vias ([board evidence §1](notes/tracemaker_board_evidence.md)). On seven failing boards, **139 of 349 sampled open connections route alone on the final board, most with 0 to 2 vias**. They stayed open because the run ran out of budget or never negotiated, not because the search could not change layers. Copper of other nets within 2 mm of a pin is the main blocker: removing it rescues **155 of 288** of the harder cases. That copper is mostly tracks. Removing only the nearby foreign vias rescues 46, and **every via rescue also works when only the tracks are removed and the vias are kept**. The "go the other way first, through several vias" pattern the user saw is real. Aleste's `/RAM/MA1` routes 217.5 mm with 4 vias to span a 25.4 mm gap. But only a whole-board window finds that path, and cheaper vias do not: they rescue 40 connections against 78 for plain extra work. The user's idea to "widen and nearly randomise" belongs on the router's discrete choices (which neighbours to rip, in what order, how wide a region), not on the A\*'s cost weights. That is what Freerouting's last-mile and neighbourhood rip-ups and tscircuit's racing solvers randomise. The measured ceiling sets the stakes. Search-only changes can close at most 44 % of the sampled open connections. Local rip-up raises that to 85 %. The last 15 % are proved enclosed even with the whole board and any number of vias.

## TraceMaker already searches every layer; its open connections fail for other reasons

The user's second hypothesis was that many open connections would route with two vias and a short run on another layer, and that the router fails to find this. The search itself does not bear it out. The lattice A\* is a full (x, y, layer) search. A layer change is never confined to special cells. For large windows the heuristic is a multi-layer cost-to-go field with via steps priced the same as in the A\*. It ignores only this run's routed copper, which is what keeps it a lower bound. The pre-check flood and the late reachability proof allow every planar move and every via landing the A\* allows. So **a connection reported "boxed in" had no lattice path at all, on any layer and with any number of vias, against the copper present at that moment** ([board evidence §1.3–1.5](notes/tracemaker_board_evidence.md)). The literature gives no better tool to adopt here. VLSI detailed routers such as Dr. CU and TritonRoute also run 3-D A\* with wire, via and violation costs ([ASP-DAC 2019 Dr. CU](https://www.aspdac.com/aspdac2019/archive/pdf/10C-1.pdf); [OpenROAD drt docs](https://openroad.readthedocs.io/en/latest/main/src/drt/README.html)). No source found in this research publishes a stronger layer-aware admissible heuristic than the per-layer, via-aware field TraceMaker already builds ([search notes, Q1](notes/multilayer_search_negotiation.md)).

The measurements show where the open connections do come from. The seven boards of the 0.12.0 release run (tiers B–D, 120 s, eight variants) ended with 526 open connections. **229 were "boxed in" in the strict first pass, 133 found "no path in window", and 152 were never attempted before time ran out.** On TCKB, mini_mass, Aleste and kitspace_d20 the run spent zero expansions in negotiation. The earlier M20 C5 study found the same on 17 of 33 failing boards, which held 1,156 of their 1,383 open connections ([doc 05 §55](../../../docs/05-routing.md)). When each open connection was routed alone on a copy of its final board with all copper locked, the results fell into a ladder of what each kind of change could at most achieve:

| What the connection needed to route alone | Connections (of 349 sampled) | Cumulative ceiling |
|---|--:|--:|
| Nothing: routes as it is (A, fast bends or the winning variant at 2 M work) | 139 | 40 % |
| More search: 20 M work with a whole-board window, cheap vias, or half pitch | 14 | 44 % |
| Foreign vias within 0.5–2 mm removed | 43 | 56 % |
| Foreign tracks within 2 mm removed (vias kept) | 100 | 85 % |
| Both removed | 1 | 85 % |
| Nothing works: enclosed even with the whole board and any vias | 52 | — |

Source: [board evidence §3.2](notes/tracemaker_board_evidence.md). Aleste and kitspace_d20 are deterministic samples. The counts are upper bounds, because one connection alone on a locked board is easier than all of them together.

The 139 that route as they are match the user's "would route easily with two vias": their routes use 0 to 2 vias in 37 of the 61 cases the plain run (A) found. They were left open by ordering and budget. That covers never being attempted, being ripped and never routed again (eight on LeeChee), or being boxed in by copper that a negotiation pass would have moved if one had run. The connections that need more search do not need "two vias and a short hop". The 78 that the 20 M whole-board run rescues have a median of 4 to 12 vias per board and a length 1.13 to 1.67 times the straight distance. **The long detour that heads away first, which the user saw on the PoE switch, is exactly this class, and the strict pass's two windows (2 mm and 6 mm plus a quarter of the length) cannot hold it.** Only the 20 mm negotiated window or the whole-board window can, and the strict pass never uses them ([board evidence §1.4, §3.3](notes/tracemaker_board_evidence.md)).

The PoE board itself could not be reproduced as the user described it. A copy of WholeBoard's rev16 switch was routed with the 0.12.1 binary. Its open connections were four GND pads dropping to the GND plane, not ordinary signal traces. Two of them, C119.2 and U10.84, are enclosed by WholeBoard's locked copper at every pitch and setting tried, including 25 µm pitch and a 30 M whole-board search. Rule 6 forbids moving that copper, so those two are input problems, not search problems. The other two, C115.2 and C123.2, are 1.17 mm stubs that need no via at all. They failed because a pad-to-plane search runs with h = 0, no field and no late proof. It floods to its 3 M expansion cap, while the same pads route in a few thousand expansions when the plan targets nearby copper of the same net ([board evidence §5](notes/tracemaker_board_evidence.md)). The live run's binary or state differed from this copy, so the user's observation is not refuted. It is unconfirmed.

This bears directly on the via-retry change now uncommitted on the `via-retry` branch (D153). After a failed search it searches the connection again at once, strictly, with vias at a quarter of their price and the busiest layer twice as dear ([router.cpp working tree](../src/route/router.cpp)). The evidence predicts where that retry can and cannot help. A miss proved "enclosed" does not depend on prices, so a retry cannot succeed. A "no path in window" miss gets the same window, so cost changes alter the order of expansion but not whether a path exists there. Only a miss that hit the expansion cap can be rescued, because cheaper vias reach a via path sooner. The study's CHEAP run (0.3 mm vias, whole-board window) rescued 40 connections, all of which extra work alone also rescued. On srj20 a via cost of 1.0 lowered the clean rate from 40 % to 26 % ([doc 16 §11](../../../docs/16-bga-breakout-plan.md)). Gating the retry on the cap miss keeps its cost off the other two classes.

## Copper near the pins blocks escapes, and its tracks block as much as its vias

The user's first hypothesis, that a via of another net placed too close to a footprint's pins blocks their escape, is half right. Open pins on dense packages do have closer foreign vias than connected pins of the same packages: **a median gap of 0.54 mm against 0.82 mm, with 45 % of open pins under 0.5 mm against 30 %**. The effect is strong on CoreOne, where U801 is a 0.4 mm-pitch xCORE and the median gap is 0.31 mm against 0.65 mm. It is strong on kitspace_d20 too, and absent on MonApollo. Yet the via is never the only cause. All 43 connections rescued by removing nearby vias also route when only the tracks are removed and the vias stay. Removing tracks within 2 mm rescues 154 of the 288 hard cases, against 46 for vias within 2 mm. A via near an open pin is the visible end of another net's fan-out stub, and the stub blocks the escape as much as the via does. Most blocking vias belong to nets with a pad on the same part, such as the package's own supply drops and dog-bones, so the space is contested by neighbours rather than taken by passing traffic. Re-routing the open net first and then the via owners (SWAP1) succeeded in only 5 of 17 tries ([board evidence §3.2, §3.5](notes/tracemaker_board_evidence.md)). Two CoreOne cases show both outcomes. `Net-(R621-Pad2)` at the KSZ8081 routes once its neighbour's D1V2 via moves, and the neighbour still routes, so that via could have gone elsewhere. `Net-(R892-Pad1)` at U801 routes with one via removed, but the five owner nets then lose two connections, so that space was simply oversubscribed.

The literature explains why coordinated pin access matters so much. It also shows that the coordination is about access as a whole, not vias alone. Kahng, Wang and Xu's pin access oracle builds about three DRC-checked access points per pin and picks mutually compatible patterns across neighbouring pins by dynamic programming. On ISPD-2018 it took **failed pins from 31–7,816 per design to 0**, and final DRCs on one hard case from 755, the best published result, to 2 ([Tao of PAO](https://vlsicad.ucsd.edu/Publications/Conferences/377/c377.pdf)). Bayless, Hoos and Hu model each candidate via as excluding the ring of neighbouring lattice nodes on every layer it spans. Solving vias and traces together saved one or two signal layers on 13 of 23 commercial BGAs. They credit the gain to routing adjacent pads on the top layer, which "left channels open at lower layers" that through vias would otherwise have blocked ([Bayless et al. 2016](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/isd/Projects/monosat/monosat_bga_iccad2016.pdf)). A PathFinder-style negotiated escape router fully routed 7 of 14 industrial cases, the same as Cadence Allegro ([NCER, ISQED 2010](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224132327_A_negotiated_congestion_based_router_for_simultaneous_escape_routing)).

The open-source routers handle this with costs and ordering, never with a keep-out. Freerouting keeps fan-out vias at least 2.5 mm from the pin centre by default. It protects short pin-to-via stubs with a rip-up cost factor of up to 20,000 × (half-width/length)², and lets that protection lapse once rip-up costs pass twice their starting value, so negotiation can clear them late ([MazeRipupResolver.java](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting/blob/2eba6f7fbf7c0ca7b5aa5f8cba7136d066fb4d10/src/main/java/app/freerouting/autoroute/maze/MazeRipupResolver.java); [DefaultSettings.java](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting/blob/2eba6f7fbf7c0ca7b5aa5f8cba7136d066fb4d10/src/main/java/app/freerouting/settings/sources/DefaultSettings.java)). Its own code comments also record the opposite failure. On a two-layer board with two 0.75 mm BGAs, eager fan-out drilled 417 vias and left 20 connections open, against 2 with no fan-out, so it now turns fan-out off or routes direct exits first on such boards ([TwoLayerBga.java](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting/blob/2eba6f7fbf7c0ca7b5aa5f8cba7136d066fb4d10/src/main/java/app/freerouting/autoroute/pipeline/TwoLayerBga.java)). tscircuit penalises traces and vias near the endpoints of connections not yet routed in the same capacity node. It also caps how many layer-changing routes a region may take by how many vias fit there ([HighDensityHyperParameters.ts](https://github.com/tscircuit/capacity-autorouter/blob/e703f0499188f858b2b5e571ac80091d4c10733a/lib/solvers/HighDensitySolver/HighDensityHyperParameters.ts); [getTunedTotalCapacity1.ts](https://github.com/tscircuit/capacity-autorouter/blob/e703f0499188f858b2b5e571ac80091d4c10733a/lib/utils/getTunedTotalCapacity1.ts)). None of the open tools reviewed charges foreign vias for sitting in another footprint's escape region during the main route, which is a real gap in prior art ([router notes, Q2](notes/existing_routers.md)).

TraceMaker has already tried the nearest cheap version of this and lost. A "pin halo" of 2 pitches per step within 0.3 mm of another part's pad went from 1,383 to 1,394 open connections ([doc 05 §55](../../../docs/05-routing.md)). The halo charged tracks. A via-only cost in the pin's outward cone was never tried. The escape plan already refuses foreign vias on reserved 2 mm corridors, but it runs in only two of the eight variants. The lesson is that **a sequential router cannot protect space for a pin it has not routed yet without starving the pins it routes first**. The way out the data supports is to free the space once a pin is proved stuck. Ripping the neighbour's connection removes its stub and its via together, which is exactly the combination the experiments show is blocking. A via keep-out stays a cheap secondary test for the two boards where it shows.

## Widening and randomising pays on the router's discrete choices, not on its search costs

The user's third proposal is to widen the parameters a lot and nearly randomise them when the router is stuck on its last few traces. The instinct is sound. Its usual target, the A\*'s cost weights, is the wrong one. An optimal A\* in a fixed window finds a path if and only if one exists there. Changing the via price, a layer's price or the bend rules changes which path wins, not whether one is found, except when the search hits its expansion cap. TraceMaker's own record says the same. Weighted A\* at 1.5 dropped srj20 to 4 % clean. Via costs of 1.0, 1.5 and 5.0 all did worse than the default 3 mm. Layer-direction penalties cost completion on ordinary boards (quick tier 89.2 % → 86.8 %). And **24 seeded variants on one hard srj20 board all ended at 33 to 40 of 52 connections**, so more draws from the same distribution did not finish it ([doc 16 §10–11](../../../docs/16-bga-breakout-plan.md); [doc 05 §40](../../../docs/05-routing.md)). In the via study the cheap-via run rescued a subset of what extra work rescued, and half pitch rescued 3 ([board evidence §3.3](notes/tracemaker_board_evidence.md)).

What the measurements do reward is a larger window, more budget on the right connection, and moving other nets' copper. Those are the choices the established routers randomise and escalate. PathFinder's central move is to let the first iteration share resources freely, so every net gets a path and congestion becomes data for history costs. It became the basis of every FPGA vendor's router ([Lafayette PathFinder page](https://sites.lafayette.edu/cadapps/main-page/pathfinder-fpga-routing-algorithm/); [UW CSE news](https://news.cs.washington.edu/category/news/page/231/)). Freerouting's end game is targeted rather than global. `LastMileBlockerRipup` removes up to 24 foreign traces and vias inside the bounding boxes of the remaining airlines, once 8 or fewer connections are open after 3 passes without progress, at most twice ([LastMileBlockerRipup.java](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting/blob/2eba6f7fbf7c0ca7b5aa5f8cba7136d066fb4d10/src/main/java/app/freerouting/autoroute/pipeline/LastMileBlockerRipup.java)). `RetryNeighbourhood` rips a radius that grows from 1.5 mm to 12 mm around open pins, with 3 contenders at first and up to 12 later ([RetryNeighbourhood.java](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting/blob/2eba6f7fbf7c0ca7b5aa5f8cba7136d066fb4d10/src/main/java/app/freerouting/autoroute/pipeline/RetryNeighbourhood.java)). From the fourth rip-up pass, Freerouting multiplies each obstacle's detour ratio by a seeded random 0.5 + r², so the choice of which net yields varies deterministically ([MazeRipupResolver.java](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting/blob/2eba6f7fbf7c0ca7b5aa5f8cba7136d066fb4d10/src/main/java/app/freerouting/autoroute/maze/MazeRipupResolver.java)). When the loop still stops with opens, it re-runs the board under named strategies such as `short-escape` and `open-escape` (escape window up to 1,000 mm) and keeps the best result ([RoutingFallback.java](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting/blob/2eba6f7fbf7c0ca7b5aa5f8cba7136d066fb4d10/src/main/java/app/freerouting/autoroute/pipeline/RoutingFallback.java)). tscircuit races up to about 100 parameterised intra-node A\* solvers and gives iterations to the most promising ([blog-post.md](https://github.com/tscircuit/capacity-autorouter/blob/e703f0499188f858b2b5e571ac80091d4c10733a/docs/blog-post/blog-post.md)). That is randomisation over solvers per small region, not over one global cost.

TraceMaker already has two of these pieces: a seeded portfolio of up to 64 variants, and transactions that accept a net loss of k connections with probability exp(−k/T) ([doc 05 §40–41](../../../docs/05-routing.md)). It also has a rip-up option close to Freerouting's neighbourhood retry. `--lns-open` lifts the neighbours of an open connection, routes it first and re-routes them in seeded orders. It was **never reached at 50 M work, because the earlier passes used the whole budget** ([doc 16 §10](../../../docs/16-bga-breakout-plan.md)). Reserving budget by a fixed share has lost every time it was tried. Stopping the strict pass at 70 % went from 1,383 to 1,536 open, capping strict searches at 100,000 expansions gave 1,422, and `--polish 0.3` closed 0 or 1 of 3 to 6 connections while losing more in the shortened main phase ([doc 05 §55](../../../docs/05-routing.md); [doc 16 §11](../../../docs/16-bga-breakout-plan.md)). **So the open question is when to trigger rip-up, not whether rip-up is a good idea.** The trigger the evidence points to is a proof. When a strict search proves a pin boxed in by this run's routed copper, the neighbourhood rip-up should run then, on that pin, with the budget that would otherwise go into flooding. D143's crowded-source start already makes that proof cheap: 2,126 expansions against 395,412 on a pin in a 3 × 3 grid ([doc 05 §55](../../../docs/05-routing.md)). Inside that rescue, the "nearly random" part should be a seeded, escalating draw over victim order and rip radius, accepted through the existing transaction rule. Determinism is preserved because the draws are counter-based.

## Planning layers and vias ahead of routing is promising but unproven on these boards

The structural idea with the best published evidence is to plan where each connection changes layer before routing. Haas (arXiv, October 2026) uses a CP-SAT model that chooses crossings and layer changes for a whole memory bus at once, then negotiates audit findings back as history prices. Every rung of two real BGA-to-DDR3 ladders came out connected and DRC-clean, with fewer vias than the human designer (80 against 88, 62 against 100). The same toolkit without the bus step left nets open on 4 of 10 rungs with 2.0 to 4.7 times the vias, and Freerouting 2.4.1 completed none ([arXiv 2610.35601](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.35601)). This is a single author's preprint on two board families, at 3.6 to 6.8 times the runtime. TraceMaker's own srj20 comparison points the same way. tscircuit's solution of sample 100 uses 168 vias for 52 connections against TraceMaker's 86, with long straight runs on both layers. The doc concluded that a planner deciding layer and via sites per connection before routing is "still the untried candidate", since via cost, package penalties, layer assignment and guides did not stand in for it ([doc 16 §10](../../../docs/16-bga-breakout-plan.md)). **This is the steelman for the user's layer-awareness instinct: it belongs in a planner that fixes via sites and layers before the search, not inside the search.** Ozdal and Wong's simultaneous escape and layer assignment, and the claimed 39 % via reduction of its high-speed follow-up, sit in the same family ([ICCAD 2004](https://static.aminer.org/pdf/PDF/000/282/915/simultaneous_escape_routing_and_layer_assignment_for_dense_pcbs.pdf); [TCAD 2008 record](https://scholars.hkbu.edu.hk/en/publications/simultaneous-escape-routing-algorithms-for-via-minimization-of-hi/)). So do the pattern-based fan-outs of commercial tools such as Altium Situs ([Altium Fanout Control](https://altium.com/documentation/altium-nexus/pcb-routing-rule-fanout-control)).

Topological and learned routers offer less evidence for closing hard boards. In a rubber-band sketch, a two-via detour is a small change that a lattice A\* reaches only through a long expansion, which is why SURF-style routers make rip-up cheap ([SURF, DAC 1991](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/127601.127622)). TopoR's advantages appear only as vendor and encyclopedic claims ([Wikipedia: TopoR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TopoR)), and the open rubber-band router Topola has published no results ([NLnet: Topola](https://nlnet.nl/project/Topola/)). On PCBWorld's real KiCad boards, PPO leads on small boards (0.86 clean) but falls to 0.45 on medium ones, where Freerouting holds 0.78. GPT-5.4 driving a routing engine gets 0.00 clean on medium boards ([PCBWorld](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05915)). DeepPCB's self-reported mean completion of 95.6 % hides a hard tail: only 72 % of 4-layer boards with 201 to 500 airwires reach 95 % ([DeepPCB benchmark](https://deeppcb.ai/pcb-autorouter-benchmark-2026/)). TraceMaker's own model-driven agent arm tied its scripted control at 968 of 968 connections ([doc 11, M18](../../../docs/11-roadmap.md)). Even Freerouting, after adding all its fan-out, escape and fallback machinery, completes 69 to 70 % of its dense tier C and at best 18 % of the 22 stress boards ([Freerouting benchmarks](https://github.com/freerouting/freerouting/blob/2eba6f7fbf7c0ca7b5aa5f8cba7136d066fb4d10/scripts/benchmark/results/benchmarks.md)). No tool examined closes boards like TraceMaker's failing set reliably. A planner is worth building only after the cheaper end-game changes show what is left.

## Ranked changes for a "finish the last connections" milestone

The ranking weighs the measured ceiling, how directly the evidence supports each change, and the effort given machinery TraceMaker already has. Effort: **S** means days, reusing existing code paths. **M** means a new code path with its tests, including a CPU reference path and an equality test where an accelerated or indexed path is involved (rule 3). **L** means a research milestone. Every change runs behind an option, must be byte-identical with the option off on `quick` and `mid`, commits only through transactions that improve the score (rule 4), budgets in work units (rule 5), and never rips locked copper (rule 6).

The evidence argues against several other changes. A new layer-aware heuristic duplicates the existing field. Cheaper vias everywhere give a subset of what extra work gives. Moving vias after routing does not help on its own, because the stub blocks as well, though it works inside item 1 when a via moves with its stub. Weighted A\* and random cost portfolios have lost, and more seeds of the same variant plateau. Learned or LLM routers are not a completion engine on this evidence. The one exception, a planner, is item 8.

To measure the milestone, first copy the `viastudy` harness and its scripts from the speed worktree's git-ignored `build/via-layer-study/` into the repo. Then re-run it after each change, so every board-level number comes with a per-connection account of which class moved: boxed in, no path in window, not attempted, or budget. A reasonable gate for the milestone is at least 15 % fewer open connections on `pcbench-bd-fail` at 10 M work with KiCad judging, no clean board lost on `pcbench-bd-clean`, `quick` and `mid` byte-identical with the options off, and `quick` run once at the end with them on. Each change should also report the work its new step consumed, since every past failure on these boards came from budget moved to the wrong place. Results go in `bench/records/<milestone>/` with the tables summarised in doc 05.

## Conclusion

The investigation turns the user's picture around. The stuck connection that looks as if it needs "two vias and a hop" usually has that path already, on the final board. It was abandoned, or it was walled in at the moment it was tried by copper that nobody went back to move. The router does not lack spatial awareness of its layers. It lacks a reflex: when it proves a pin enclosed, it moves on instead of clearing the space around that pin. Every budget-share experiment so far has failed for the same reason. Work was moved by a clock or a percentage rather than by a proof, so the extra effort went to connections that could not use it. Triggering rip-up on an enclosure proof is the cheapest change that matches the evidence. It also gives the user's "widen and randomise" idea a principled target: escalate the radius and the victim order around one proved-stuck pin.

There are also limits that no amount of search will remove. About 15 % of the sampled open connections, and two of the PoE switch's four, are enclosed even with the whole board, any number of vias, and every option tried. Some of those are enclosed by locked input copper, which the router must not move. The run report should say so plainly ("enclosed by locked copper on all layers"), so that users stop reading those as router failures. The remaining 85 % are reachable in principle. The order in which to go after them is: the pin rescue, then the end-game retry, the window, and the pad-to-plane fixes. Only the residue that survives all of those should justify the larger bet on a via-site planner.
